# 1crypt.txt - (dq0) a stand-in theme: the crypt of a dead king.  The first of the themes (their files' names in
# order: the first is the one the tests' small seeds pick, theme.h), and the one the dungeon was grown by before
# themes were data: its strata's styles, its rooms' words and its light the same, all the bestiary in it.  make.js
# makes the themes the game's lib/dq0/dq0t.dat (theme.h's struct theme a theme, its words after), which it reads
# as it starts.  The real themes are the user's to write; these are for the engine.
#
# A line a field, a key and its values between bars; a line that starts with a space goes on with the line before
# (its last value, a space between; or, its first character past the spaces a bar, more values).  The keys:
#   theme     its name, as the story and the endings say it
#   mission   escape, rule, or both: what ends the game well (the way out opened and taken; the place ruled)
#   story     what the character knows of the place as it comes in (told after the title)
#   stratum   a stratum, from the top down (8 at most; past the last, the last again): its name, and the styles its
#             plan may be grown in, one or two letters (b built, n natural, m mine, x mixed), one picked by its seed
#             (the top stratum's first); and its purpose, what its builders built it for (DUNGEON.md: built to a
#             plan): halls, keep, temple, cellars, crypt or works (halls if it's not given), and fortified or open
#             after it (a keep's fortified, unless it's open; the rest open, unless they're fortified)
#   rooms     the eight words for a room, by its kind (the growers': a chamber whose use is lost, a hall, cells, a
#             vault, a crypt, a gallery, a cave's chamber, a storeroom)
#   uses      the twelve words for a built room's use past those (a kitchen, a pantry, barracks, an armoury, a
#             chapel, a bedchamber, the lord's solar, a well room, a cistern, a gatehouse, a guardroom, a workshop), if
#             the theme has words of its own for them
#   light     a room lit by its own light: by what
#   denizens  the bestiary's kinds that live here (dq0d/kinds.txt's keys, spaces between), or * all
#   kind      a kind of thing of its own (8 at most): its name (13 letters at most), its ways (letters: h held in a
#             hand, t in both, w worn, g hangs from a belt, c a container, s a surface (things lie on it), f fixed
#             where it stands (never taken up), p its name's a plural; - none), its weight (pounds, to a quarter:
#             63 and three quarters at most, more taken as that), its bulk (pints, 255 at most), how far off it's
#             made out past a speck (feet, in fives, the map's squares: 5 a coin or a key, 20 a sword, 35 a chest,
#             75 at most), its material (wood iron steel bronze leather cloth bone clay stone glass silver other),
#             the craft that knows it (weapon armour metal wood hide cloth pottery jewel book food bone stone glass),
#             its worth (pennies); a container's or a surface's room (pints) and the weight it bears (pounds)
#   piece     a piece of the puzzle (or a fixture of it: an altar, a throne), put down in the dungeon (8 at most):
#             its kind, the strata it may lie in (first-last, from the top: 0), concealed (how hard to find by
#             searching: the steps worse a search goes, rs.h's scale, 0 to 30: 2 tucked away, 6 hidden well, 12
#             cunningly) or - in plain view, how many
#   rule      what's done with the pieces (8 at most): read X | words; combine X + Y = Z | words (both to hand:
#             they become Z); put X | Y | effect | words (X put on Y, or in it); out X | effect | words (X used on
#             the way out); the effect - nothing more, open (the way out opened), rule (the place ruled: an ending)
#   lock      the way out, shut: told as it's tried, or looked at
#   opened    the way out, open
#   ending    escaped, ruled or died | its words
#
# And its history (games/dq0/HISTORY.md): what the dungeon is what's left of.  A hand-written theme may give any of
# it, or none; a game's own theme is themegen's (games/themegen), which writes all of it, and fills in what a
# hand-written one (dq0 -t FILE) leaves out.  The record keeps what the engine builds by (theme.h); the rest is the
# file's, to read.
#   seed      the seed themegen made it from (for the record)
#   region    the land it lies in: its name; its land (mountains, hills, forest, marsh, coast, plain); its rock
#             (granite, limestone, sandstone, basalt, slate, chalk); its water (springs, river, lake, sea, none); its
#             climate (cold, mild, warm, wet, dry)
#   people    a people of its history (8 at most): its name (15 letters at most), what they are (dwarves, men,
#             elves, kobolds ...: the game's own), their tongue (namegen's, 1-8), how they build (hewn, dressed,
#             brick, timber, rough)
#   era       an age of its history, oldest first (6 at most, numbered from 1): its people (a people's name), what
#             they made (keep, temple, mine, tomb, town, hold), how it ended (abandoned, conquered, collapsed,
#             flooded, cursed; or held, theirs still), how long ago (centuries), what was left (nothing, stores,
#             bones, treasure, the dead, a curse), and its words (what happened, as the clues tell it)
#   layer     a stratum's history, oldest first (3 a stratum at most; a stratum without any has one, built to its
#             stratum line's purpose): the stratum (0 the top), the era (0 none said), what befell it (built,
#             ruined, reused), and how: built or reused, its purpose (as a stratum's: halls, keep ...; the uses'
#             plan its last); ruined, how (abandoned, conquered, collapsed, flooded, cursed).  A stratum's age is
#             its oldest layer's era's, if its layers say one (else the dungeon's dice)
#   faction   the inhabitants now (8 at most): its name (11 letters at most); its kinds (dq0d/kinds.txt's keys,
#             spaces between); its strata (first-last, from the top: 0); about how many on each of them; its leader
#             (a kind's key, or -); its lair's stratum; its attitude to the character as it first meets it (h
#             hostile, u unfriendly, w wary, i indifferent, f friendly); and how it stands with the others ("name
#             h/w/f, ...", as dq0d/factions.txt had them).  The denizens' (popgen's); without a denizens line, the
#             factions' kinds are the ones that live in it
#   goal      what the game's for (themegen's goal kinds, games/themegen/goals.txt): artifact, evil, prisoner or
#             through; the artifact's, the evil's, the prisoner's or the far exit's name; its stratum (a far exit's:
#             a way out there, open from the start); its guardians (a faction's name, or -)
#   and a piece's line two more values, if it has them: its era (it lies among that era's works: 1 ...; or -,
#   anywhere) and its guardians (a faction's name: their lair by it, popgen's; or -).
# The theme checker (make.js's, themegen's) holds every theme to it: every goal reachable (each rule's things to be
# had, the mission's ending a rule can bring about), every faction placed (each one's kinds living in its strata).

rule     | read | stone tablet | The tablet is cut with the old letters: "Here lies Aldric, king under the hill.  His
           seal was broken at his death, the sun's half from the moon's: made whole, it opens his door to the
           living.  Who sets his crown upon his throne keeps his house for ever."
rule     | combine | sunstone + moonstone = king's seal | You fit the two stones together: they close with a click
           into one, the king's seal, whole again.
rule     | out | king's seal | open | You press the seal into the hollow in the black door.  It sinks home; somewhere
           behind the stone a weight falls, and the door grinds open on the stair up to the daylight.
rule     | put | iron crown | throne | rule | You set the iron crown upon the throne.  The dust stirs; all through
           the dark the dead turn their faces towards you, and are still.

lock     | The way out is shut fast. Whatever opens it is somewhere below.  A door of black stone closes the stair,
           a round hollow in its face.
opened   | The black door stands open on the stair up to the daylight: go up to leave.
ending   | escaped | You climb the stair into the light of day, and behind you the hill closes again over the Crypt of
           the Dead King.  You have escaped.
ending   | ruled | The crypt is yours, and all who lie in it.  Aldric's house has a new master, under the hill, for
           ever.
ending   | died | The crypt keeps you, as it keeps its king.
