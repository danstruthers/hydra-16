# The game: a critique of its design, consistency, architecture and parser, and what to do next

A companion to ENGINE-CRITIQUE.md (the engine) and COMMANDS-CRITIQUE.md (the command path). Those two planned the
infrastructure, and most of it is now built. This one asks what the player gets from it:
- the game's design;
- how consistently its rules are applied;
- the architecture as it now stands;
- the parser's complexity.

It ends with how to make the game simpler and more consistent, and what to do next to make it more enjoyable and
playable.

**How it was made** (2026-11-16, at c7a8c5b7, all 460 tests passing). Six reviews were run:
- five read the code: the parser and commands; the world; the minds, senses, referee and sound; generation, the story
  and the words; the architecture, build, tests and docs;
- one played the game: eight games and about 430 commands at the turns pace, as a weak scribe, a knight, a sage and two
  guardsmen, in generated and hand-written dungeons, plus fifteen of the soak runner's random games.

Every finding was cited to a file and line or to a transcript, and the main ones were checked again:
- the iron key;
- the garbled refusals;
- the grammar's veto;
- the denizens' taking;
- the help's buffer;
- the strata's sizes;
- the test switches;
- the lines quoted from play.

Counts are close, not exact. Paths are relative to reborn/games, except tests/ and sim/, which are relative to reborn/.

**Out of scope:** the between-dungeons work (the town, the scholar's house, the account, prices), which is on hold.
Where a recommendation touches a decision the user has made, it says so and names the DECISIONS.md entry.

## 0. The verdict

**What's right, and should be kept:**

- **The architecture.**
  - The tasks are split by concern, with one writer for each region of the hub. Every test checks it (120,067 writes
    checked, no violations).
  - Perception comes first.
  - One intent shape serves the character and the denizens.
  - The turns pace is deterministic and can be replayed.
  - Placement is generated by the build.
  - Each shared fact is stated once and generated for every language (hubgen.js, msgs.def, records.def).
  - Narration is data, there is one record store, and the paged ROM library holds the shared code.
  - The referee's skeleton is proved by the null ruleset.
- **The depth of the simulation:**
  - light and darkness;
  - sound, smell, and traces on the floor;
  - bearings underground, and the sky seen through skylights;
  - wounds, bandages, shock and tiring;
  - rope, digging and wells;
  - the denizens' day, run by the clock;
  - a bestiary that is data.
- **Much of the writing.**
  - The room prose.
  - The walking: "You go through a doorway. The passage turns left. The passage forks here."
  - Bandaging.
  - The check before you leave with the goal undone.
  - The "why you came" lines: "A shepherd found the stair and wouldn't go down it; you would."

**What's wrong comes down to four things:**

1. **The depth doesn't reach the player.**
   - The player can't reliably say what they mean. Every noun phrase is cut to one word, so "unlock the chest with the
     iron key" answers "Your iron won't do for a lock."
   - The player isn't told what they need. The help is printed as one block, with words split across lines. A
     generated goal's pieces lie at random in strata up to two miles across, with no signposts.
   - The player is rarely rewarded:
     - every container the maker places is empty;
     - the generated puzzles are one or two steps, with a hollow climax;
     - the denizens' morale, factions and speech hardly show.

   In two ordinary games of 206 commands the play-tester met no one and found no way down.
2. **Rules are applied unevenly.** A rule is often written for one actor, one kind of thing or one verb, and not for
   the others:
   - the character's diagonal step costs 7/5, but a denizen's doesn't;
   - denizens take what the character has hidden, through walls and in the dark;
   - doors and lids are two closure systems with different formulas;
   - "all" means something different to take, drop, put and take-from;
   - a miss is worded five ways, by the kind of thing missed;
   - the tests play a game with seven switches the player never has.
3. **State is kept consistent by hand.**
   - Fields mean different things by context: 10 of the item record's 19 fields do.
   - Zero means "none" in fields where zero is also a value. That caused the portcullis bug.
   - Sentinels collide across fields.
   - 45 macros give one variable's bytes another name.
   - The minds' state is 43 parallel arrays, reset by hand, and one of them is missed.
   - The 8K bank window is managed by hand, and nothing checks it. Ten commits in 27 days fixed wrong-bank bugs; the
     last was a crash found by chance.
4. **Fitting decides the design.**
   - Ten bytes decide whether "side pocket" can be a word.
   - The grammar's meaning table is at 255 of 255 records, so new verbs read the raw line again instead.
   - The cycle budgets ratchet tighter than the noise that moving code causes, so code is copied and data reordered to
     pass them.

   About a third of the commit-message text since 2026-11-11 is about room and cycles.

None of this calls for a rewrite. Each problem has a contained remedy, and most of the remedies are small. The order of
work (section 10) runs:
1. a cheap safety net first;
2. then the player understood;
3. then a dungeon with something worth finding;
4. then denizens with something to say.

The structural simplifications ride along with the features that need them.

## 1. What a player meets

This section is from the play test unless noted. Replies are quoted exactly.

### The opening

**The opening works.** A generated theme says clearly why you came and what you're after: "Somewhere below, in the
Temple Crypt, lies the Mask of Vethoul ... you came for it. The way back up is open behind you." The room prose and the
walking read well. In 430 commands and fifteen soaked games, nothing crashed or hung.

### The first hour

**Naming things is the biggest friction.**
- "Which door?" came up 13 times.
- Adjectives are ignored. With "a door ahead (closed, locked) and one to your right (open)" in view, "go through the
  open door" answered "You head for the door. The door is shut: open it first."
- The verb doesn't filter the choices: "open the door" offers the open one too.
- Relative names drift as the character turns. "open the door to my left" turns the character, so the next "the door
  to my left" is another door.
- Carried things win over things in view. "open the case" lifts the carried bolt case ("The bolt case has no lid.")
  while a furniture case stands in the room.
- Answering "Which door?" with another command drops the question without a word.

**The hands give up the light.** To free a hand, the game puts down the lit lantern or torch, not the thing that has a
home.
- "hold the sword" gave "You set the lantern down at your feet. You take the sword in your left hand." The knight then
  walked into the dark, with no warning that the lantern was left behind.
- "break the door" with no tool named batters with the lantern: "You set to battering the door with the lantern." It
  broke an iron door an inch thick in two minutes.
- "bandage my thigh" laid the torch down, and the next "rest" let it burn out.

**"look" shows only what's ahead**, by the user's "Only what the character can see" (DECISIONS.md:368-373). In the
middle of a room, facing a wall, the look was "You are in the middle of a bedchamber, by the light of your lantern. The
ceiling is flat." Nothing else was told, though the room had a door, the way out, a skylight and a case. "look around"
gives the same.

**Visible bugs in the first hour:**
- "take all from the case" gave "You find no #17 in the case." every time, and "in the shelves" for a surface.
- "drop the candle stubs in the pit" gave "You reach for your backpack.", then an old inventory, cut off.
- The help splits words across lines: "the we / ll".
- "Which one?" gave three cave adders the same letter (G).
- "combine the tablet and the seal" gave "Combine what with what?" twice.
- "1 feet long" and "lie (F) something small".
- A generated history with "aNd" and "ago. the Khulim dwarves".
- "Above, it's dark: it's night.", followed by an ending "into the light of day".

**Common phrasings fail.**
- These all answered "I don't understand that.":
  - "remove the torch from the backpack";
  - "pick the lock" (the help offers it);
  - "climb on the table";
  - "search under the table";
  - "eat" alone ("drink" alone works).
- "drop all" gave "Drop all what?", and "push the wall" gave "Push the wall what?".
- "put the torch on the door" gave "The torch won't hang from a belt."
- "open the drawer", with no drawer about but a door ahead, gave "It won't open: it's locked." It worked the door.
- `say hello` is refused without the quoted form being shown, though the user decided it should be shown
  (DECISIONS.md:617-626).
- There is no "undo"; "g" and "again" work.

**Too many words in some places, too few in others.**
- Lighting a torch in the dark printed "You strike a spark. / It doesn't catch." 49 times.
- Every take or stow through the pack is four to six lines: "You take off the backpack, and set it down at your feet. /
  You hold the backpack open. / Taken. / You put on the backpack."
- One fight command printed about 30 lines.
- "Taken." and "Put down." don't name the thing.

**Harsh, with no explanation.**
- "kick the door" broke a foot ("a serious fracture to your right foot (S2)"). The door's iron is told only when it's
  examined.
- After a dart trap, the character "struggles, but can't get up" through seven tries and eight hours' rest. Nothing
  says why, or what else to try.

### Reward and progress

**Reward and progress are thin.**
- **Empty containers.** All seven pieces of furniture opened were empty. So is every chest, sack and hidden compartment
  the maker places: dq0m puts nothing inside anything except a sconce's torch (sconce.c:74).
- **Locked doors.** Of eleven closed doors tried, six were locked and two barred; styles.txt:47-49 makes half of a built
  stratum's closed doors locked or barred. The scribe's start room had one door, locked iron, and the scribe had no
  picks.
- **No way down, no one met.** In two ordinary games of 206 commands, the play-tester found no way down and met no
  denizen. The soak runner already notes that one game in forty meets anyone in sixty turns (sim/tools/soak.js:7-8).
- **Tension.** The only tension came from injuries the character caused itself.
- **The journal.** The one piece on the top stratum is a journal. A sage with two scripts failed to read it, and the
  game didn't say what tongue it was in.

### Talk and the puzzle

**Talk is a mood roll.** With a friendly dwarf:
- "talk to", "greet" and "threaten" all answered "The dwarf miner seems friendly already: that won't move it further."
- "ask the dwarf about the king" gave "The dwarf miner isn't your ally."
- "how much for the pick" gave "The dwarf miner has no pick to sell." while it held one.
- Persuasion can be repeated until it works, since no attempt is remembered (dq0d/dq0d.c:4129-4192).
- Killing a sleeping dwarf made the others merely "unfriendly".

**The puzzle's own words aren't commands.** The crypt's way out is "A door of black stone closes the stair, a round
hollow in its face."
- "put the seal in the hollow" gave "You have no hollow to hand."
- "use the seal on the door" gave "You can't see how to use the seal."
- Only "use the seal on the way out" works.

Once it's solved, the game says "That's what you came to do. Now to get out alive." The ending shows a character sheet,
with no account of the run.

## 2. The parser: complexity and consistency

Much of COMMANDS-CRITIQUE.md's plan is real: steps 1-7, A-L and 6b. That includes handles, one name rule, one
"which?", the dispatch table with its coverage report, and refusals as rows.

Two of its pieces were never built:
- step 8, the grammar's roles;
- section 4.1's noun dictionary, with affordances.

Most of what follows comes from those two gaps. ENGINE-CRITIQUE.md's row 4 says "done", which overstates the parser
side.

### 2.1 A line's journey

"put the dagger in the sack" passes through about twenty stages, in three tasks and a dozen overlays and parts. In
order:
1. The aliases (main RAM, overlay 4, part 9).
2. The split into several commands (overlay 6).
3. The designators (overlay 22).
4. The grammar task, dq0c: its tokenizer and alias trie, then the grammar's yes or no, then the meaning scan.
5. Names (overlay 10, parts 5-6). The line is posted again if a name changed it.
6. The pace words (overlay 16, part 4).
7. s_command (part 2), which runs in turn:
   - the pending choice;
   - the parts;
   - travel;
   - the grammar's veto;
   - the hand phrases (part 23);
   - the body places (overlay 14);
   - the resolver (overlays 22, 11 and 24);
   - the row's refusals (overlay 11);
   - the puzzle (overlay 5);
   - the handler (part 2, 29 or 13, or overlay 1).
8. The first words (overlay 20).
9. The world's chooser.
10. The words (overlay 24, then the renderer's overlays 27-30).

Three readings of the line run side by side, and none uses the others' structure:
- **The grammar** (dq0c/cmdmatch.s) answers only yes or no; its derivation is thrown away (cmdmatch.s:11-14). Its no is
  a veto. "remove the key from the chest" is refused, although the meaning scan understood it (VB_RETRIEVE_OBJECT, the
  key, from, the chest).
- **The meaning** (dq0c/cmdmean.s) is a scan of the words in any order, with about 24 patch-up rules keyed on 31
  constants (cmdmean.s:88-119, 979-1253).
- **The UI reads the raw line again** in 38 places:
  - 32 strstr tests in 12 files: "wash", "sharpen", "kneel", "dive", "short", "until", "watch", "dead", the hide's
    " behind ", " in ", " under ", and others;
  - six dedicated readers: the hand phrases (main.c:1586), given_to (errand.c:268), the places (sway.c:572), worn.c,
    pace.c's lists, and "around".

  The earlier critique counted about eight.

### 2.2 Every noun phrase is cut to one word, by rules that disagree

- **The rules.**
  - The first noun phrase keeps its last word before a preposition; the second keeps its **first**. The code is
    cmdmean.s:961-966, and its host twin sim/tools/commandwords.js:197-199 (`&& !word2`).
  - Qualifiers are dropped, except for nameis.s's 13, which are read again from the raw line for the first noun phrase
    only.
  - A room-part word always sets the first noun phrase's kind, whichever phrase it was in (cmdmean.s:933). The second
    phrase's kind is never set.
- **The iron key.** "unlock the chest with the iron key" sends the tool as "iron", and resolve.c:297's
  `strstr (w, "key")` answers "Your iron won't do for a lock." Every key in the catalogue is an "iron key", and the look
  calls it that, so players will type it. This was confirmed in play.
- **The torch on the door.** "put the torch on the door" arrives as {door, "torch"}, with no second thing. Given no
  "into", the world's stow means the belt: "The torch won't hang from a belt." This was confirmed in play;
  COMMANDS-CRITIQUE's section 2 lists it, still unfixed.
- **The rope and the pillar.** "tie the rope to the pillar" drops the pillar and ties the rope to the nearest holder
  (hthings.c:471-475).
- **Qualified names.** "the kite shield" reaches the world as "shield", and "the stone chisel" as "stone", which also
  names sling stones and the whetstone. So the user's stone-versus-wood chisel (2026-11-03) can't be named reliably.
- **Two-word names.**
  - "the side pocket" was read as the side of the room plus "pocket", and it crashed the game until 2026-11-16. It is
    now one literal rule (resolve.c:402-411).
  - "the secret drawer" is read as the plain drawer, which is why the desk's is called a "secret niche".

### 2.3 Choosing which thing is meant

This is the play test's commonest friction (section 1). The resolver:
- doesn't read state words: open, closed, locked, barred, broken;
- doesn't filter by what the verb can do: open offers open doors, and go through offers locked ones;
- reads "to my left" by the facing now, not the facing when the player saw it;
- prefers carried things to things in view: the bolt case, or the pack's sack over the room's.

The user's rule for doors is a numbered list unless one is directly in front (DECISIONS.md:362-363). Every remedy below
keeps it and only shortens the list.

### 2.4 The vocabulary is scattered, and its tables are full

- **The word files.** commands.ebnf's terminals, .words, .verbs and .aliases hold 708 distinct words. 492 of them
  appear in two or more files, and 51 in three or more.
- **The aliases.** 85 of commands.aliases' 153 entries repeat commands.verbs, some with a different meaning: "stow"
  means take off in one and stow in the other, and the same goes for "wear" and "pack".
- **Adjectives.** Three adjective lists disagree: the grammar's 21, the UI's 13, and the meaning's 15 selectors.
- **Lists in code.** About ten more word lists live in code:
  - pace.c's 30 or so words;
  - sway.c's places, whose "bottom" clashes with "false bottom";
  - the hide places;
  - the hand phrases;
  - errand.c's skip words;
  - way.c's coins, grille and spy-hole words.
- **Literal tests.** About 62 literal tests on a noun's word sit in 12 command-path files. "well" is known in 10 files,
  "bar" in 11.
- **Capacity:**

| Table | Used | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| The meaning records | 255 | 255: the next verb with a row of its own fails the build |
| The dictionary | 718 | 784 |
| The rules bank | 7,664 bytes | 8,192 |
| dq0c's RAM tables | 14,176 bytes | 14,592 |
| The resolver's overlay | 140 bytes free | |

The comments say the records' ceiling is why "wash" and the grapples read the raw line again (commands.verbs:95-97,
187-190).

### 2.5 The special cases are three missing mechanisms

There are about 94 word-specific tests in the command path, and nearly all are symptoms of three missing mechanisms:
1. **No noun dictionary built from the catalogue**, with multi-word names and qualifiers as data. Symptoms:
   - the side pocket;
   - the little-window and spy-hole rules (way.c:299-304, hplaces.c:74-89);
   - money and coins as pennies (way.c:199);
   - "stone" for sling stones (main.c:1792);
   - the case and the bolt case; the kite shield and the tower shield;
   - the floor-place words.
2. **No roles from the grammar.** Symptoms:
   - the 32 raw-line reads;
   - the hand phrases, given_to, and the hide's locatives;
   - the bar rules;
   - the 46 floor and wall phrase verbs (commands.verbs:496-541).
3. **No affordances in the catalogue.** Symptoms:
   - "key" and "pick" for locks (resolve.c:297);
   - the well, rope, bar, pane and shutter in the refusals (actions.c:235-236);
   - "water", "food", "remedy" and "waterskin" by name (feed.c, hthings.c);
   - the digging tools by name (main.c:2045-2058);
   - the denizens' wants by name (dq0w/wtake.c's `strcmp (CATALOGUE[k].tool, "food")`).

### 2.6 "Left to the handler" means garbled

**The coverage report.** It has 2,074 cells: 91 are done by handlers, 417 refused by their rows, and 1,566 left to the
handlers. Its columns are a word plus the sixteen kinds of room part. No column covers what most lines name:
- a carried thing;
- a thing lying about;
- a fixture;
- furniture;
- a compartment;
- a denizen;
- oneself.

**What the left cells do.** Ten were traced:
- drop, push, pull, taste or use with a room part answer "<the whole line> what?". For example: "Push the wall what?"
  (refuse.c:151 copies the whole line when the reason is past U_YET).
- "hide the stairs" hides the character.
- "open" with a word that names nothing works the door ahead.
- "put" onto a feature goes to the belt.
- "tie" to a pillar ties to the nearest holder.
- 97 cells say "... in this game yet".

**The sweep can't see it.** The sweep test only checks that the thing's word appears in the reply
(tests/suites/dq0/parser.js:440-443), so "Push the wall what?" passes. It never tries a line naming two things.

**The same miss is worded five ways, by the kind of thing:**
- "You see no X here." (things);
- "You haven't seen a X here." (features, choose.c:235);
- "There's nothing like that here to fight.";
- "You see no one here.";
- "You have no such thing."

The side pocket gets the first on a look in, and the second on "go to".

**"all" has four meanings:**
- "get all" takes furniture into the pack, and the dwarves' tools from under their noses.
- "drop all" answers "Drop all what?" with two things in hand, and with two others drops just one thing.
- "put all in the sack" asks "Which thing?".
- "take all from the case" prints a placeholder.

### 2.7 Two semantics, one unused

sim/tools/commandcompile.js (1,310 lines) holds a reading of the corpus by roles (phraseSem, 115 cases), and 669 corpus
cases are checked against it. The game doesn't use it; the game uses the scan.

The two disagree:
- "put the coin in the side pocket": the host says there's no such thing;
- "lie on the bed": the host gives no action.

So the corpus checks a meaning that players never get, and the game's own meaning is held only by phrases.txt's 155
cases.

Adding a verb touches about eleven files: .ebnf, .verbs, .actions, .corpus, actions.c, dq0.h, a handler, cmds.h,
said.txt, help.txt and commandcompile.js. Today it is also blocked by the 255 records.

### 2.8 What a simpler parser looks like on this machine

The target, cheapest path first:

1. **One noun-phrase rule** in cmdmean.s and its twin.
   - The head is the last content word of each phrase, and the word before it is kept as a qualifier.
   - A room-part word that isn't the head is a qualifier ("side pocket", "glass shards").
   - Each phrase gets its own kind.
   - The name is sent as "qualifier head" (it fits the 16 bytes), and `named` matches each word against the thing's
     name.
   - This deletes resolve.c:402-411, nameis.s's quals and given_to.
2. **A wider meaning table**, with a two-byte record index.
   - Then the verbs that read the raw line get rows of their own.
   - The hand phrases, the body places and the locatives become word classes.
   - This deletes the 32 strstr reads, the hand phrases' part, the hide's locatives and worn.c's reader.
3. **One noun phrase in the grammar**, with an optional locative, merging `<object>` and `<thing>`.
   - Add the missing forms:
     - remove X from Y;
     - climb on, sit in;
     - get in, out, on or off; out; in;
     - empty X (onto Y);
     - look under or behind (a search of that thing);
     - "<compass> wall" anywhere.
   - Pay for the bytes by deleting the 46 floor and wall phrase verbs.
4. **One generated vocabulary.**
   - commands.verbs owns the verbs; the aliases file becomes the player's shortcuts only.
   - One adjectives section feeds the grammar, the qualifiers and the selectors.
   - Nouns come from the catalogue's names, the compartments' nouns, the room parts and the denizen kinds.
   - A portal's grille, spy-hole, pane and bars become part nouns.
5. **Affordances as catalogue data.** A flags byte for each kind: lock tool, rope, tie point, climbable, water, food,
   dig tool, light. This deletes about 40 literal noun tests.
6. **A resolver that reads like a player.**
   - State words are honoured.
   - Choices are filtered by the verb: closed doors for open, locked ones for unlock, passable ones for go through.
   - What the last look named, or what's ahead, is preferred over what's carried.
   - "to my left" is read by the facing at the last look.
   - A pending question is kept until it's answered or dropped aloud ("Never mind the door, then.").
7. **Every cell answers by design.**
   - A kind with no word gets "You can't push the wall."
   - A feature the verb can't use gets "You can't put the torch on the door."
   - A word that names nothing gets "You see no drawer here."
   - One wording for a miss, for every kind of thing.
   - One meaning of "all" for every verb: each thing named, and furniture, worn things and others' things skipped.
   - The coverage report gets columns for the kinds of thing; the sweep fails on any "... what?" to a line that names a
     thing, and tries lines naming two things.
8. **Then decide the grammar's future** (a question for the user). Either:
   - make it record roles (step 8, in assembly), and port phraseSem's cases to it; or
   - once 1-7 are in, delete phraseSem and hold the corpus to the game's own meaning.

   Keeping two host semantics is pure cost.

## 3. The world's rules

### 3.1 One path for every actor: true for doors, not for bodies

wdoor.c really is shared. The character and the denizens open, close, lock, pick and force by one path, with only 14
branches on who is doing it. Bodies are not shared:
- **Step time.** The character's cost() adds 7/5 for a diagonal (dq0w/dq0w.c:477, 992, 1594, 7429). wbody.c:255-293
  copies the formula without it, and the minds do take diagonals, so a pursuer gains about 40% on a diagonal passage.
- **Taking.** s_den_take (dq0w/wtake.c:206-262) scans every thing in a 9-by-9 box and never checks I_HIDDEN, light or
  line of sight.
  - A thing the character hid (a minute's work and a Conceal test, things.c:5810-5829) is found by a robber within four
    squares, through a wall, in the dark.
  - That empties the "hide" verb against the very robbers it exists for, and goes against the user's rule that verbs
    do real things.
- **Robbery** takes coins from anywhere on the character, closed or locked containers included (wfight.c:589-600).
- **Fumbling** is tested only for the character. A denizen's take is a flat two seconds (wtake.c:259).
- **Perception** (from the minds' review):
  - **Facing.** A denizen's eyes have no facing (dq0p/sense.c:139-141), while the character sees only its front half.
    Nothing can be crept up on from behind.
  - **Lights.** Denizens carry no light ("the denizens' lights, to come", dq0w.c:8217), though a dwarf miner carries a
    lantern. The player never sees a patrol's light coming.
  - **A torch from afar.** sense.c:16-17 says a denizen sees the character's torch far off, but nothing calls that
    code. A torch is seen only within the kind's sight (12 squares at most), not the 31 that DUNGEON.md:1084 gives.
  - **The grille.** The character sees through a grille; a denizen doesn't.
  - **Hearing.** Denizens hear each other straight through rock (dq0d.c:1704).
- **Known and decided**, for completeness:
  - denizens don't spring door traps (the user's "left for now", HOUSE.md:3758), though robbers now pick locks
    (wport.c:13's comment says they never do);
  - a denizen opens a portcullis at once;
  - denizens know nothing of compartments.

### 3.2 Two closure systems

Doors (wdoor.c, struct portal) and lids and drawers (things.c's s_shut_work) each implement open, close, lock, unlock,
pick and force, with different formulas:

| | A door | A chest |
|---|---|---|
| Picking | at the lock's steps (wdoor.c:139) | -1 - quality/3 (things.c:3790) |
| Forcing | material, locked or barred, rot, newness (wdoor.c:168) | wood and quality only (things.c:3831) |
| Noise | 7 | 6 |

A rotten door is easier to force; a chest has no rot at all. One table of closure kinds (time, skill, the terms that
modify it, noise) could serve both, through two adapters for where the state is kept. That would make them agree and
halve the code.

### 3.3 Costs are literals

ENGINE-CRITIQUE's section 3.1 asked for an action table "with costs". It was built only on the UI's side, for dispatch
and refusals. In the world:
- 31 busy() sites have literal times;
- several constants are defined in more than one file: FORCE_T, FETCH_HANDS, SEARCH_R, ROPE_REACH, HW_SEEN, NWHICH and
  others.

A world action table (time, skill, noise, fatigue, whether it can be interrupted, the tool) makes a new verb mostly a
row, and keeps similar verbs' timings consistent.

### 3.4 Freeing a hand

The user's rule (2026-11-03, DECISIONS.md:1748): put the other things away "in their appropriate place" before taking
a tool in hand. A torch goes into a sconce or is laid on the ground; a sword goes into its scabbard.

The rule says where each thing goes. The code (things.c:1170-1180's s_room) frees whichever hand is "next in the way",
which in play is usually the light.

The fix keeps the rule and only chooses the order: first the hand whose thing has a home (scabbard, pouch, belt, pack),
the light last. It is a refinement, not a reversal. With it:
- coins go into the purse;
- a light left burning is picked up when a walk starts, or the game says "You leave your lantern behind.";
- nothing is battered with a light.

### 3.5 Overloaded state and sentinels: the bug traps

- **The item record** (dq0/items.h:282-314). At least 10 of its 19 fields change meaning by where the thing is or what
  kind it is:

| Field | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `x`, `y` | a square; or the actor; or the holder plus `IC_MARK \| prep << 4 \| compartment` |
| `owner` | a faction; or a denizen id's low byte; or a rope end's height |
| `hide` | how well hidden; or a sconce's lit flag; or a denizen id's high byte; or the kind a rope is tied to |
| `fuel` | a light's minutes; or a holder's compartment lock nibbles; or packed rope state |
| `size`, `thick` | a measurement; or a key's door id |
| `shut` | IS_NOCKED and IS_BURST are both 0x08 |

- **Zero as "none" where zero is a value.**
  - act.work's 0 means none, but it holds PA_* values, and PA_OPEN is 0. That was the portcullis bug, fixed by
    substituting a value (dq0w.c:4324).
  - "Plus one" appears 59 times as the workaround.
  - "Who" fields disagree: 0 is the character in wk.who and cmd.actor, but means none in pc_foe, act.foe and grip.
- **Colliding sentinels.**
  - act.work's LW_TRACE, LW_REACH and LW_HIDE are the same three bytes (0xFF down to 0xFD) as wk.what's W_STEP, W_GONE
    and W_BLOW, defined across four files.
  - About 30 range checks on enums of categories (IO_HOLD..IO_TAKE, k >= LT_STUDY, what <= PA_UNLOCK) would silently
    change meaning if an enum were reordered.
- **45 storage aliases.**
  - 33 are on iq, the copy of the UI's request, for the rope, the well and the drag.
  - Six are in the reach (rs_hit is lx, rs_want is lw, and so on). They survived the step planner, whose record says
    "no more aliased scratch bytes".
  - The drag's four date from 2026-11-10, so this is still the default pattern.
- **Hidden parameters.** Compartments added about 14 globals that are set around calls, for example
  `wcn = 1; n = at_hand (iq.name); wcn = 0;` (things.c:3707). There are at least 14 such sites.
- **The minds.** There are 43 per-denizen arrays, and enlisting resets 9 of them (dq0d.c:1231-1236). drawn[] and
  fooled[] are never reset. So a hostile denizen enlisted into a dead one's slot can inherit "drawn on" with its target
  zeroed, and walk toward square (0,0) for up to eight decisions. This was found by reading, not seen in play.

None of these is wrong today in the cases tested; each is a trap for the next change. The remedies are mechanical:
- hubgen gives every enum stored in a "0 none" field a NONE member at 0;
- one header for the sentinels;
- generated category tests, such as IO_IS_HANDLING (k), in place of the range checks;
- an accessor named for each overload (KEY_DOOR (p), DEN_ID (p), ROPE_END (p), COMP_STATE (p, c)), with a table of
  meanings by where the thing is and its kind;
- a named union of per-feature scratch structs for the aliases: the same bytes, but the overlap visible to the
  compiler;
- one struct per denizen in the minds, cleared with one memset when enlisted.

### 3.6 The big files are organised by part, not meaning

dq0w.c (8,307 lines), things.c (7,531) and dq0d.c (6,254) are laid out by swapped part:
- one part's code is spread through the file (dq0w.c's SW2CODE at lines 1140, 1240 and 2264);
- intents are dispatched by code size: main RAM, then part 53, then part 37;
- the comments are largely a log of placement. dq0w has 927 "part N" mentions and 461 dated notes. 42% of dq0d.c's
  characters are comments, with 165 "part N" notes, some naming a part that isn't the one in effect.

Placement is generated now, so splitting the files by meaning costs nothing in bytes:
- **dq0w.c:** the clock and scheduler, walking, timed and door work, fighting, digging, climbing, travel, the body,
  lights.
- **things.c:** records, containment, the reach, handling, closures, choosing, describing, searching, kit.
- **dq0d.c:** records and placing, the core, hunting and hiding, wants, the day, morale, social, allies, work.

Hand-made copies that ENGINE-CRITIQUE listed are still there: trap_at21, near_foe12, put_down63, and_last36/62,
kept71, room_lit71 and span67/68. The Chebyshev distance is written 13 times across the tasks.

## 4. The denizens

### 4.1 What works

The bestiary is data (kinds.txt: 29 kinds by 36 columns), and behaviour follows the K_* flags. The denizens' day
(sleep, meals, watches by thirds, work, leisure) runs by the clock, so their routines can be learnt, and the stir words
show them: "Two soldiers are ahead, 20 feet away, at dice." They work doors and take things by the character's own
code.

### 4.2 What doesn't reach play

- **They never fight each other.**
  - dq0harn/fight.c:665-668 returns no outcome when both sides are denizens ("later, with allies").
  - The minds only ever attack the character (dq0d.c:2218).
  - keep_off runs before ally(), so an ally steps away from your foes, and hostile factions step politely round each
    other (dq0d.c:1898-1921, 2109-2115).
- **Talk has no memory and nothing to say.**
  - influence() remembers no attempt, a plain failure costs ten seconds, and persuasion goes as far as ally. So
    repeating it wins anyone over (dq0d.c:4123-4192); a test types "befriend the rat" three times.
  - "ask X about Y" is parsed with its topic, then sent to plain talk (actions.c:90), which drops the topic.
  - No denizen ever tells the player anything.
- **Morale and attitude don't show.** Morale is modelled in depth (bonds, tenure, succession: about 550 lines), but it
  shows only as "It flees." or "rallies its fellows". A look gives a denizen's doing, never its attitude.
- **The leash** (inferred). A hostile denizen chases only within 2 x range + 8 squares of its lair. At the edge it
  stands watching, 6-12 seconds a decision (dq0d.c:1933, 2158), so a bowman can shoot it without reply.
- **Only eight act at once.** Any more near the character are frozen and unseen (dq0d.c:1292-1303).
- **Sound plays less than the screen tells.** dq0s drops every world event a denizen causes (dq0s.c:343). So these are
  told in words but silent:
  - a denizen opening, slamming or breaking a door;
  - winding a portcullis;
  - raising the alarm;
  - snoring.

### 4.3 The referee

**The boundary is mostly clean**: the null ruleset runs 76 meaning tests. It has leaks:
- dq0harn reads the hub's actors, the character's square, light, gait and stance, and the senses' sight bank, and it
  writes pc_hunger (dq0harn.c:705-742, 855-884);
- the shared skeleton parses HarnMaster's weapon versions and bandage cloths (rf/rf.c:101-105);
- the world hard-codes HarnMaster's launcher ranges by catalogue order (wshoot.c:69-73).

Fights, the most rule-heavy part, have one test under the null ruleset out of 45.

**HarnMaster's numbers reach the screen:**
- "UP n" is always on the status line, and "UP 0" reads like "up";
- each wound has its code: "(S3)";
- conditions read like "in shock: 11 injuries and lesser hurts, the worst a serious bite ...; penalties: universal N,
  physical N".

The plain words (minor, serious, grievous) are already there, and the null referee shows the wording is the referee's
to choose. Whether to keep the numbers on screen is the user's call.

## 5. The dungeon and the story

### 5.1 The goal

**Only three rules.** Every game a player starts with a clock seed gets its goal from themegen, and those goals use only
read, take and put (goals.txt:38-253). The engine also supports combine, "out", and goals with two solutions, and
themec checks them, but they appear only in the three hand-written themes the tests use.

**How often each goal comes up.** By histgen's odds (histgen.c:404-421), a goal is:
- an artifact, about 42% of the time;
- evil, 25%;
- a way through, 25%;
- a prisoner, about 8%.

Lore and scourge goals come only from rumours, which are on hold.

**The climax is hollow.**
- The prisoner is a fixed surface you "put the key on". She stays "a chained woman", while the ending says she walks out
  behind you.
- The guardians are compiled and checked but never placed (theme.h:220). HISTORY.md:181-182 lists them as still to
  come.
- The cursed dead never lie still.

**The help, the title and the goal disagree.**
- The help teaches the crypt's puzzle, not the generated ones (help.txt:12).
- The title screen promises "a puzzle in pieces ... find them, and you may yet leave the place", yet a generated
  dungeon's way out is open from the start.
- Nothing tells the goal again: no command repeats the story, and a resumed game doesn't (theme.c:105-108).

The user has said the game "is about solving a puzzle" (DECISIONS.md:1720).

### 5.2 Size without guidance

**The sizes are dice.** Below the top, a stratum's side is (dq0w.c:1801-1822, commented "a stand-in for the theme's
say"):
- 4-8 squares a quarter of the time;
- 60-199 squares about a third of the time;
- 400-1,999 squares, up to about 10,000 feet, four times in ten.

Five regions in six are rooms.

**Nothing guides the player.**
- The one or two ways down and each puzzle piece are placed at random, evenly over the stratum (plan.c:373-392,
  440-451).
- The number of denizens doesn't grow with the area (popgen.c:398-424).
- The only bearing is a draught across the stratum toward the way down (dq0p/sound.c:156-181), and the help never
  mentions it.

The user wants strata of any size, and their own example ties a stratum's size to what it is: "One may be 200 ft.
square, and then next might just be a storm cellar, 10x10, with a ladder down to the catacombs that is miles in each
direction" (DECISIONS.md:88-92). The problem isn't the size. It's that the size comes from dice rather than from what
was built there, and that nothing guides the player through it.

### 5.3 Rooms and reward

- **One role far from the way in.** A chunk's role is its distance from the way in, capped at four (uses.c:211), so
  every chunk beyond is the same role. In halls that role is cells: 19 of 57 distinct first looks in the test scenes
  are in a "cell".
- **Marks and junk.** Each use's mark lies in the exact middle of its room. 54 of the 144 kinds of thing left lying
  about (37.5%) are potsherds, bones, skulls or broken swords.
- **Every container is empty**, apart from a sconce's torch. That includes:
  - the one chest in three that the world locks;
  - the hidden compartments that furniture gets half the time;
  - the nearest chest, which a found key with no door to fit is fitted to.
- **Rot.** Rot strips useful things from older, deeper strata, but it skips caves and chambers, so a 4,000-year-old cave
  has fresh rope.

### 5.4 Flavour built and never shown

- **The peoples.** peoples.txt's walls ("of black stone set with niches of bones"), doors, marks and lights are built
  and put on the card, and nothing reads them. Its peoples also disagree with history.txt's: the Delvers speak
  "delver" where the dwarves speak khuzdul.
- **The deities.** Only their names reach the parser.
- **The eras.** An era's "what was left" is written, but not compiled.
- **Murder holes** are set, and never read.
- **Room materials** never reach the look.

### 5.5 Generation's own consistency

- **Two ways of placing denizens**, with two meanings of a faction's number:
  - histgen writes a stratum's lairs;
  - popgen spreads the number over all the faction's strata;
  - the hand-written themes use kinds.txt's lairs instead.
- **The old fixture dice.** They are keyed by chunk coordinates alone (place.c:99-108), so a given room's trap, windows
  and door states are the same in every game. The reason was byte-identity, and it lapsed when furniture changed every
  dungeon.
- **Many sources of randomness:** 13 legacy hash formulas, two streams, and several one-offs.
- **The room uses.** The 20 uses are positional in about eight places: five tables in uses.c, place.c's furniture,
  kinds.txt's home letters, and the theme format's fixed word counts. Adding a library or a forge touches every theme
  file.

### 5.6 The words

**The voice is consistent:** second person, present tense, plain, British. Many lines are good: "The kobolds pick your
bones, and argue over your boots."

The weaker parts:
- **The look repeats itself.** Every room look has a ceiling sentence; 35 of 57 first looks say "The ceiling is flat."
  Fixed things "lie": "lie (B) a well and (C) some shelves".
- **People are "it":** "The elf unslings its hartbow." Whether people should be he, she or they is the user's call.
- **Odd lines:**
  - "Quicker, the giant rat backs away from you."
  - "You attempt to batter the door with the rope, but it seems ineffectual."
  - "Hunger and thirst: not hungry, not thirsty."
  - "1 feet long".
- **Text bugs:**
  - History clues are cut at 239 characters by a 240-byte buffer (linegen.c:107, 514): "...and their dead would not
    lie s".
  - Three help paragraphs are longer than help.c's 400-byte buffer, and are split mid-word.
  - A recent seeker's journal is written in the oldest people's tongue (read.c:132-149).
  - "down there, in the Barrows", when the Barrows is the top stratum.
  - Night above, then "the light of day" in the ending.
- **The help** is 19 dense paragraphs printed at once, and "help puzzle" prints them all. It never mentions:
  - eat, sleep, tend, wake;
  - fill, dip, wash;
  - shoot, load, engage, charge;
  - gather, swim, pray;
  - knock, push, pull, touch, taste;
  - sit, crouch;
  - copy, rip.
- **Words in C.** About 290 places in the UI still build words in C rather than in said.txt.

## 6. Architecture: banks, room and time

### 6.1 The bank window

Each task has one 8K window, shared by the code overlays, the hub and the data banks.
- The world maps the hub back by hand after any call that might have moved the window: about 850 hub_map calls.
- About 680 accessor calls move the window without saying so.
- Each function's contract about the window is a comment.

**The bugs.** Ten commits in 27 days fixed wrong-bank or layout bugs, eight of them in the world:
- a denizen "striking with its fists", because its weapon was read through the wrong bank;
- a reach's time written into a chunk;
- event words written into the item bank.

The latest (79083a98) was a crash: "look in the side pocket" returned into the hub's bytes as code, and wrote into an
overlay's code. It was latent for eight days, and was found by a probe, not a test.

**Also unchecked:**
- The swap stack holds 12 entries and is pushed with no bound (swap.inc:51, 226), while pack.js estimates the world's
  call depth at 29.
- The C stack has 768 bytes before it overwrites the part in use.
- 158 pragmas keep state inside swapped parts, which a swap loses.

**The remedy.** The cheapest is in the emulator, at no byte cost:
- fail on an instruction fetch from $8000-$9FFF unless a code bank is mapped there. That alone would have caught the
  crash at its first byte;
- flag writes to the hub header's offsets in any other bank, from code that names HUB;
- watch the swap stack against its depth, and keep a canary at the C stack's floor.

Then sw_call can restore the caller's bank when the caller is in the window, and the build can use pack.js's call
graph to flag overlay calls into functions that move the window.

### 6.2 Room

| Where | Free | Floor or limit |
|---|---|---|
| The UI's (dq0) main RAM | 3,043 | 3,000 (the build checks only 2,048) |
| The world's main RAM | 2,285 | 2,048 |
| The referee's main RAM | 2,067 | 2,048 |
| The world's parts | part 35: 0; 58: 1; 59: 1; 24: 2; 86: 2 | each of 3,648 |
| The world's overlay banks | 14.1K of 256K; the largest gap 1,725 | half a part |
| The catalogue bank's code | 242 (6,158 used) | 6,400 |
| The grammar's meaning records | none (255 used) | 255 |
| The phrase-row overlays 28 and 29 | 1 and 6 bytes | |

**The ceilings shape rules and words:**
- a magic-hidden door can be found by knocking, because a part had ten bytes;
- a door's poison needle is "not yet: part 5's room";
- "side pocket" can't be a world word, because the kinds' bank is ten bytes over;
- compartment state went into `fuel`.

**The largest block of main RAM in every task is the C library:**
- 6.3K in the UI;
- 5.2K in the world;
- 5.0K in the minds;
- 12.6K in the referee, 7.4K of it HarnMaster's own library.

The build's "under 512 free" warning lists about 90 items every time, so nobody reads it.

**Structural ways to raise the ceilings, instead of squeezing:**
- move the referee's HarnMaster library into its overlays;
- split s_pace_cmd: it is 4,339 bytes, and it is why the UI's swap area is 4,352;
- move the kinds' name words out of the kinds' bank;
- generate the overlay side of placement, as the parts already are. Today it is 166 hand-written overlay stubs, 91 .cfg
  lines, and an overlay count kept in step in three files;
- consider C code in the paged ROM, as the line and the record store already are. It needs a fixed runtime contract
  like lnzp.inc, costs about 120 cycles a call, and removes the window hazard for that code. The cost is re-flashing
  for updates, which is the user's call.

### 6.3 Time

**The targets.** The user's targets (D17) are a first letter within 720,000 cycles and a look within 1.79M
(tests/lib/dq0.js:864). Around them grew ratchets:
- dq0answer's per-command budgets, "brought down ... to just over what's measured ... never raised"
  (dq0.js:844-857);
- dq0traces' step: 1.8M, then 1.9M, 1.95M and 1.92M, with no player target behind it;
- the start's budget, raised to 245M, though the user said the start's time isn't a concern (DECISIONS.md:3197).

**Layout noise.** The measured costs move with memory layout alone: "the world's BSS moved six bytes and dq0traces'
step cost 1,922,509". So the ratchets fail on layout noise, and code is shaped to pass them:
- a coder copied into four parts (things.c:115-117);
- phrase rows reordered (`last SA`);
- a static kept only to hold the BSS layout;
- timing scenes run with furniture off, until a later commit won the cycles back.

**The remedy.**
- Fail only on the user's targets, and on a silence at the start.
- Record the rest as trends, with an alert on a rise of more than 5%.
- Add a headroom policy: when a part or bank is under 64 bytes free, or a measure is past 90% of its target, room is
  won back before the next feature.

### 6.4 Determinism

- **The turns pace is meant to be the same game whatever the tasks' timing** (hub.h:1098).
  - Yet twelve tests on the accepted noise list run only at the turns pace, and still differ between builds.
  - Two races (dq0reachstop, dq0tiring) were accepted rather than fixed; the user accepted that on 2026-11-11
    (DECISIONS.md:3799-3802).
  - A lockstep would end both: the UI takes the next line only when the world, the minds and the senses are idle. Then
    every turns-pace test can be held to an exact transcript, and the soak's -same check can run across builds.
- **The real-time paces are not reproducible, by design.**
  - The minds run as a background task and decide whenever they get the CPU.
  - All the minds share one dice stream.
  - The world applies a step when it reads it.

  So a bug seen at the real pace can't be replayed. Two changes would bound the noise: keying the minds' dice per
  denizen (as the maker's dkey does), and applying steps at their intent's time (as blows already are). A bounded
  lockstep, with a log of (game time, command), would make real-time games replayable, at the cost of a game clock
  that stretches slightly when the CPU is busy. That trade is the user's call.
- **The hold-back while a line is typed** is written five times, in three tasks, on two clocks:

| Task | A command | A typed line |
|---|---|---|
| The minds | 30 game tenths | 600 real ticks |
| The world | 50 ticks | 600 ticks |
| The senses | 100 ticks | 600 ticks |

The world alone uses 12 variables and 3 timers for it, and it has needed two rounds of fixing holes. At the normal
pace, a slowly typed line costs each denizen up to about 7 game seconds, since a late one may make up only 3 (inferred
from the code). One give-way module in the ROM library, on one clock and used by all three tasks, would be simpler
and fairer.

### 6.5 The task split

The split is right for the address space: each task has its own main RAM and private banks, and merging tasks would
make 6.2 worse. Its cost is coordination, as in the three hold-backs above. The hub is sound: 5,874 of its header's
8,192 bytes are used, with an assert.

## 7. Tests, build, docs and process

### 7.1 The tests play a different game

- **Seven switches.** The runner adds seven switches to every dq0 game line unless a test opts out
  (sim/test.js:178-252):
  - soft questions answered automatically;
  - no engagement initiative;
  - the reach in one lump;
  - bearings never tested;
  - the old kit;
  - an unfitted outfit;
  - a dusk start.

  203 of 310 dq0 tests opt out of none of them. With the maker's flags and seven environment switches, that's about 60
  test-only code sites in the most cramped tasks.
- **Hand-written themes.** About 85% of the tests' game starts use a hand-written theme: 645 of 756, with seed 3, the
  crypt, in 288 lines. Every player gets a generated theme. So the generated goals, populations and words are the least
  tested part of the game.
- **Fixed seeds.** 302 of 303 tests fix a seed, so every change to generation rewrites about a hundred transcripts and
  gets a new off-switch. Furniture changed 98 transcripts and added dq0furn=0 to five scenes. looks.ref has been made
  again 62 times.
- **The result.** Three of the four bugs fixed this week (the portcullis, the grille, the relight) were found by the
  user playing, and the side-pocket crash by a probe.

**The remedies:**
- **Test the game as shipped.** Flip the runner's default so the switches apply only where asked, migrate area by area,
  then delete the switches and their code.
- **Scene constructors.** A scene file read in test mode, giving the room, the things, the denizens, the kit, the light
  and the hour. It replaces the pokes, the environment switches and the dependence on seed 3.
- **Pinned themegen seeds**, one per goal kind, as the main scenes.
- **The sweep's new checks**, from 2.8 step 7.

### 7.2 The build and the cycle

- The build compiles the whole OS twice, once per serial chip, and every game in each pass, though only the BIOS
  differs.
- Nothing is cached.
- A cold suite costs about 68 CPU-minutes (597 seconds at -j 8).
- Scenes are keyed on the images, so every rebuild starts cold.

Three changes would cut the cycle sharply, at low risk since the images compare byte for byte:
- build the images that don't depend on the chip once;
- add a target that builds only the games;
- cache the compiler's outputs by a hash of their contents.

### 7.3 The docs

- **DECISIONS.md** is 6,699 lines, 4,865 of them from the last ten days. It has 73 "as built, for review" entries, open
  questions in 26 blocks under five labels, and 57 mangled section signs.
- **HOUSE.md and DUNGEON.md**, the would-be references, never mention compartments, furniture or the side pocket.
- **ENGINE-CRITIQUE.md**: a single status cell is 8,442 characters.
- There's no README for the game, and TESTING.md still describes 252 tests.
- **Commit messages.** Every one since 2026-11-11 is one line of about a thousand characters, so `git log --oneline` is
  unreadable.
- **Comments** are 43-53% of the big C files, and record dates and placement rather than invariants.

**A suggested structure:**
- games/dq0/README.md: what DQ0 is, the tasks, the data files, build and test, a glossary, and how to add a verb, a
  kind, a fixture, a denizen and a sentence.
- HOUSE.md, and a new WORLD.md (things, compartments, portals, furniture), as references to the current state, without
  history.
- STATUS.md, a line per plan item.
- OPEN.md, every open question in one place.
- DECISIONS.md for the user's decisions only; the "as built" reports kept per change.
- Commit messages with a subject of 72 characters or fewer, and the measurements in a trailer.
- Comments that state invariants.

### 7.4 The process lives outside the repo

These live in a scratch brief, not in the repo:
- the transcript hook in sim/test.js, which is never committed;
- the sentence comparer;
- the accepted noise list;
- the zero-page and fill rules;
- the memory floors.

They belong in the repo, as:
- a TRANSCRIPTS=DIR option;
- a sentdiff tool;
- a tests/noise.txt giving each noisy test and why;
- an AGENTS.md with the rules;
- per-task floors in build.js.

## 8. Simpler and more consistent: the engineering recommendations

| # | What | Simplifies or fixes | Size | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Emulator guards: fetch from the window only with a code bank mapped; hub-offset writes in other banks; the swap stack's depth; a C-stack canary (6.1) | The whole wrong-bank class, caught at its first byte | small | low |
| E2 | The noun-phrase rule, the vocabulary, affordances, the resolver, every cell by design (2.8, steps 1-7) | About 94 word tests, 32 raw-line reads, three adjective lists, the two parsers' disagreements | medium in all, in small steps | medium (many transcripts' words) |

## 12. In one paragraph

DQ0's engine is sound and its simulation is unusually deep, but the depth stops short of the player. The parser cuts
every name to one word and reads the line three ways; the resolver ignores what the player sees; the help splits its
words; the goals' pieces lie at random in strata miles wide; the containers are always empty; and the denizens'
morale, factions and speech never show. Underneath, rules are written for one actor or one kind of thing and not the
others, state is kept consistent by hand, and fitting into memory and under cycle ratchets decides more of the design
than it should. The fixes are mostly small and local:
- guard the bank window in the emulator;
- make the player understood: one noun-phrase rule, a generated vocabulary, affordances as data, and a resolver that
  reads state and verbs;
- fix the visible bugs, and keep the light in hand;
- test the game that players actually get;
- then change every dungeon once, to give it rewards, guidance and a real climax, and give the denizens something to
  say and someone to fight.
