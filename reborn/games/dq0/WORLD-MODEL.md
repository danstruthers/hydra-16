# DQ0: the world model

The rules of DQ0's world, drawn from the user's own decisions. Read this first, before any other doc.

A behaviour these rules settle is decided, not asked. Build it at the full generality the rule implies and list it in your report as an assumption, citing the rule. For example: "A flier crosses open pits too (B2)."

The numbers in brackets are lines of DECISIONS.md, where the user's own words are. DECISIONS.md is the dated archive; this page is the rule.

## 0. Precedence (when rules conflict, the earlier wins)

- **P1. Realism first.** The world behaves as a real, Harn-like, 12th-century world would (44, 1609). Where HarnMaster 3.5 speaks, the book rules. Where it is silent, the house rule is what the real world would do, not what is cheapest to build.
- **P2. One path.** Every actor, the character included, is a body that acts on the world by the same rules and the same code (2309). Actors differ only in their data: sheet, species, body, kit and mind. An asymmetry between the character and a creature is a bug, never a question.
- **P3. Generalize.** A rule stated for one case holds for every case of the same class (476). Apply it to the class.
- **P4. Verbs do what they say.** Every verb attempts a meaningful action in the world (1911). "Wear" takes only what can be worn.
- **P5. A puzzle, not a contest.** "It is not about winning or losing. It is about solving a puzzle" (1677).
- **P6. Swappable.** The ruleset owns tests, skills, harm and their numbers (1994). The world plug-in owns history, peoples, lands, deities, names, scripts and calendars (2254). Neither leaks into the other's code.
- **P7. Budgets bound how, not what.** D17, the RAM floors and the headroom rule are the engineer's to meet (2268, 2370). Only if meeting one would change what the player sees is it a question.

## 1. Bodies

- **B1. A body has:**
  - a size;
  - its ways of moving (walk, fly, swim, climb, burrow, from its sheet);
  - limbs and hands;
  - senses;
  - wounds, fatigue and a load;
  - one state of consciousness: awake, asleep, senseless, held, bound or dead.
- **B2. A body meets only what its way of moving touches.** A flier never touches the floor, so floor traps, pits (covered or open) and pools pass under it. A trap on a door or a wall still takes it (2510, 2542).
- **B3. Consciousness:**
  - The asleep hear by half and don't see.
  - The senseless sense nothing and do nothing.
  - Harm wakes a sleeper (2462).
  - Every cause of a knockout gives the same state (2467), and its shock tests are rolled as time passes (2455).
  - The state is saved.
- **B4. Harm is harm.** A blow, a trap, a fall, fire and poison wound by the same rules, and test courage the same way (2522).
- **B5. Hands:**
  - A hand holds one thing.
  - To free a hand, a body puts things home (sheath, belt, purse), the light last (2263).
  - A drawn weapon is kept in a fight (2341).
  - A light left burning is taken up on any move away from it (2343).
- **B6. The helpless** are the senseless, the asleep, the held and the bound. They can be robbed, or struck without defence, and they wake by B3 (2514, 2532).
- **B7. One clock for fatigue.** Every body tires and is burdened by the same clock.

## 2. Things

- **T1. Every thing is unique,** with its quality, materials and wear (239). Every thing is somewhere:
  - in or on a holder, by its room and by what it can hold (liquids and gases too, 207);
  - worn at a place on the body;
  - in a hand;
  - or lying on a square.
- **T2. Physics:**
  - What rests on a lid slides off as it opens (2284).
  - A thing is put only where it can rest (2354).
  - A liquid is a thing in a container (2440).
  - A spilt liquid lies and acts: oil burns and is slippery (2470).
  - Deeper in a container takes longer to reach (2474).
- **T3. Nothing vanishes.** Takings are carried home (2517); one killed on the way drops them. Food eaten is used up. Rot works by age (2259).
- **T4. What is found means something.** A key fits something near (1673). Any tool works as its form allows, and the better one is suggested (1563). Building materials are things to carry (2534).
- **T5. A creature carries what its life needs.** Folk who need light carry a light and the means to light it, leaders included (2512, 2536). Robbers carry their tools.

## 3. Places

- **Pl1. A stratum follows its history.** Its size, style, age and contents follow from what was built there, and when. A purpose-built place follows a plan. Deeper is older and poorer (2259).
- **Pl2. The denizens shape the dungeon too.** Anything an actor can do to a place, every actor can do: dig, build, brace, trap, break.

## 4. Perception and knowledge

- **S1. Only what is perceived is known or told.**
  - Sight has facing and needs light from any source, as the looker's own eyes allow; darkvision is one such pair of eyes.
  - It works both ways through any opening (2244, 2318, 2445).
  - The player is told what the character's senses report, by the same rule that tells a creature what it notices.
- **S2. One hearing rule for every sound and every hearer.** Every act makes the act table's noise (2331, 2523). Minds turn to what they hear, as their nature says (2538).
- **S3. Knowledge.** An actor knows:
  - what it has perceived: one who sees a trap found learns of it (2541);
  - what it has been told;
  - what its own people made: a faction knows its own traps and doors (2508, 2539).
  
  A written clue is in its writer's script (2261).

## 5. Time

- **Ti1. One clock.** Every act takes the act table's time, whoever acts. Typing costs nobody time; an actor behind the clock catches up in full (2242, 2437).
- **Ti2. Long tasks are one system** for every actor. They can be broken off by "stop", or by the world (476, 2347).
- **Ti3. The save is the whole state.** Whatever is in force survives a save and load.

## 6. Actions

- **Ac1. The thing named is the thing used** (1038, 2345). "Break the door with the lantern" batters with the lantern, which may break.
- **Ac2. Out of reach, walk over first** (2411).
- **Ac3. A skill test uses the skill the book names,** and marks it.
- **Ac4. Minds:**
  - What a mind chooses comes from its nature and its knowledge (2313, 2515).
  - What it can do, and how the world answers, are the same as for the character (P2).
  - Every ability built is offered to the minds.

## 7. Telling

- One wording per action, for any actor: "You take..." and "The bandit takes..." come from one row (2075).
- Nothing is told twice.
- First words come at once, in the story.
- Only seen names are used. People are "they"; beasts and the unseen are "it".
- Plain words go on the screen; numbers go on the sheet.

## 8. What the engineer decides (never asked)

- **Budgets:** how to meet D17, the RAM floors and the headroom rule.
- **Structure:** the code's structure, bank placement, data layout and tools.
- **Dice:** how they are drawn, provided every test is fair and independent.
- **Tests:**
  - A test may be re-aimed when the behaviour is right by these rules; say why in the report.
  - Never loosen a check of behaviour.
  - Tests check what the game does (state and events) before the wording.

## 9. What is still asked of the user

- Puzzle and goal design.
- Difficulty and balance.
- Presentation: the map, pronouns, letters and menus.
- The scope of content.
- The boundaries of the plug-ins.
- A conflict between two rules above.
- Anything irreversible or wide:
  - the save format;
  - every seed's dungeon;
  - the referee skeleton;
  - the hub's layout;
  - removing a feature.
- Reversing a recorded decision.

When asking, ask for the **rule**, not the case. Give the rule you would add or change and the cases it settles.
