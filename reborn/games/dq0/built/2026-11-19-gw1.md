# Time and the minds: one give-way, catching up in full, the minds' record (2026-11-19; as built, for review)

The one-path plan's stage 1 for time and the minds (ONE-PATH.md 2.6, 3 and 4; the sense audit's 3.5 and 3.6;
GAME-CRITIQUE.md's E7 and E15). Branch gw1, from reborn-commands e995c535, rebased onto e492b66a (e10, act1),
60f42c66 (speed6, bankcheck), 07d796cb (sense1) and 7df1d7f2 (speed7, the images).

Of the four parts briefed, three are built. Each denizen's own dice was built, measured and taken out again: it is
left for gw2, with sense1's two leftovers (facing, and the minds hearing another actor's noise).

## What was built

### 1. One give-way, in the paged ROM library

**The library's entry.** dq0lib's GW_HELD (modules/dq0lib/gw.s; the jump table's $A069, after act1's AC_ROW at
$A066). At the real paces it holds back a task's work while the UI's command is in hand, GW_CMD real ticks (100,
half a second), or while a line's keys go on, GW_KEYS (600, three seconds). One rule, on one clock, in one place.

**A line its own.** A line is told by the parser's seq, the UI's ui_cmd and its keys' number (ui_keys: kseq, each
line's own from its first key). The keys' number counts even for a caller that doesn't hold for keys, so a new line's
Enter is held at once, before its seq is posted. The world's ui_first used to tell a line by its seq alone: for some
50,000 cycles after an Enter, the last line's run-out hold was taken for the new one and the world wasn't held (found
by a trace of dq0letdens; the base has the same hole).

**Each task keeps its own state** in hub.h's `struct gw` (six bytes). Its flags:
- GW_PACE: the turns pace told by the hub's pace (a long wait held as well), not by tpr being 0;
- GW_NOKEYS: a command in hand alone, not a line's keys;
- GW_LOOK: a look one way's rest awaited (the hub's way_want) counts as in hand;
- GW_ALL: held for as long as anything's in hand, no limit;
- GW_REST: at the real paces, a look one way's rest awaited holds with no limit and leaves the state alone; GW_REST
  alone asks only that.

**The copies it replaces:**

| Task | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| The world's `ui_first` | its own, 50 ticks a command, a line by its seq | the library, GW_CMD |
| The world's `den_held` | its own, 600 ticks for keys; speed7's look-rest check | the library, GW_REST |
| The world's lights' job (`s_burn_job`) | speed6's condition inline | the library, GW_LOOK and GW_ALL |
| The minds' `held` | 30 tenths of the game's clock: 300, 180 or 120 real ticks by pace | the library, GW_CMD |
| The senses' `sense_held` | 100 ticks, 600 | the library |
| The maker's `giving` (its line hold) | 100 ticks, 600 | the library |

**What stays with the tasks:** the world's long jobs keep their free passes (kfree, kcut, kmore; KEY_HOLD now
GW_KEYS); the senses' own look keeps `give_way` (GIVE_WAY now GW_CMD); the maker still holds for the game's own time,
and at the turns pace while a line's in hand.

### 2. Catching up in full (the user's answer)

- **No cap on a late denizen's catching up.** The world's CATCHUP is gone (wbody.c). Before, it made up 30 tenths at
  most, and a denizen fallen behind the clock lost the rest.
- **It decides again in the same pass, at every pace**, up to 64 decisions (the turns pace's number; one a pass at the
  real paces before). The minds' give-way is asked between its decisions.
- **A step carries its time.** I_STEP:den's `when` (msg.t) is the actor's ready as the mind meant it; the world takes
  the step no sooner (wbody.c's I_STEP). It was first put in I_DEN, the head every denizen intent shares: act() then
  wrote it over I_PORTAL's step, I_TAKE's and I_ROB's thing and I_ENTER's ready (dq0fetch, dq0fetchlid, dq0denkey and
  dq0allythrow failed: an ally's errand found its thing 0). Now a step's field alone.

### 3. Each denizen's own dice: built, measured, taken out (gw2)

A stream per denizen (xorshift's state in its record, seeded as it's enlisted from the game's seed, the dice offset,
its record and the clock, stirred twelve times; swapped in as it decides). Two findings took it out:

- **The noise didn't shrink.** The 30 tests noise.txt lists for the minds' timing, the build as it is against the same

- **Twelve scenes' rolls reshuffled.** A dice offset (DQ0_ROLLS, 1 to 40) found five of them again (dq0antidote,
  dq0armour, dq0grapple, dq0hidelook, dq0lurk); seven failed the same way at every offset (dq0kit, dq0strip,

  never close; with the stream off, all seven pass). Seeding each stream from the minds' stream instead brought back
  dq0robbed alone.

Those seven scenes would have to be written again for it, so the minds keep their one stream as before, and gw2 can
take it up with the scenes.

### 4. The minds' record per denizen (E15)

**`struct mind md`** (dq0d.c) gathers the minds' 43 per-actor arrays into one record per denizen:
- who it is: kind, home, water, want;
- what it knows: its senses' last, where it last sensed the character, fooled, seen with a foe, drawn, intruded on,
  beside, sniffed, shot at;
- what it wants: an ally's order, its errand, a thing to loot;
- what it's doing: its way, steps, stalking, stay, fleeing, its day, its search, its door tries, its rounds;
- and, new, its target (the character so far; nothing reads it yet), ready for "searching is anyone's".

**Laid out by field** (md.lsx[k], not md[k].lsx): the 6502 indexes a byte array in one instruction, a record's field
by a multiply, so each field stays an array and the record is the struct whole.

**Made fresh whole** as a denizen's enlisted into an entry: `fresh` clears every field by MIND_FIELDS' table (the build
stops if the table doesn't cover the struct), then s_enlist and enlist_near set what comes from its record and its
enlisting. Before, a list of arrays was made fresh and the rest set one by one. Its one change of state: stq, the
square its stay began on, was left from the entry's last actor; now 0, the stay begun as it first steps.

**The tests** find a field by name with dq0Ad (tests/lib/dq0.js): a label, else md's place plus the field's offset,
read from the struct in dq0d.c.

## Every sentence the player sees differently

The full suite's transcripts against the base's (7df1d7f2), sentence by sentence (sentdiff): 22 tests differ, 21 of
them in the known noise (the minds' timing: dq0answer, dq0antidote, dq0letseveral, dq0letsight, dq0letwhich,
dq0torchwords and others; the OS demos), one past it:

- **dq0venom**: "You see a skylight in the roof ahead to your right, daylight falling from it." is now "... ahead to
  your right." The dusk's news ("Fading light falls from the skylight above: it's dusk.") comes after the door's
  answer, not before it, so that answer's look was taken before dusk was told. The world's time-of-day pass waits for
  the answer first.

Intended changes of timing and choice, each with its reason:
- **Denizens catch up in full.** One held back while a command's answered makes up all its time when the hold ends,
  not three seconds of it: it closes in sooner. In dq0letdens's menacing game a fourth rat now reaches the character
  and fights through attack C's answer. The test sends any rat but A, B and C off as attack C and attack A are typed
  (dq0Alone, its keep now a list too), the scene as written.
- **The minds wait half a second of real time for a command**, not three seconds of the game's clock (0.9, 1.5 or 0.6
  real seconds by pace). The world waits half a second, not a quarter.
- **The world is held from a line's Enter**, not from its seq's posting.

The minds' timing noise on the final code (one stream, catching up in full; the pad measure above): 226 sentences in
14 tests (288 by count) against the base's 147 in 14 (195). More denizens move in a given answer now, so more of them
are met at other moments. dq0letdens alone is 52 of them.

## Open questions

1. **Each denizen's own dice** (gw2): seven scenes would have to be written again, and the noise it was to shrink
   didn't shrink on the final code. Is it still wanted, and against which measure?
2. **Catching up in full raises the timing noise** (147 to 226 sentences in the pad measure): denizens held back by
   the give-way now make up their time at once when it ends. A denizen's catch-up spread over the passes after the
   hold would keep the game's time and move less at once.
3. **An ongoing fight's blow beside a new command.** The character's fight (the world's fight_on, RS_ATTACK) steps on
   while a new line's answered; the give-way doesn't hold it. In dq0letdens's menacing game it cost 837,757 cycles to
   attack A's first letter. Should the character's long task's next step take the one give-way too?
4. **Facing and the minds hearing another actor's noise** (sense1's leftovers) are gw2's.
