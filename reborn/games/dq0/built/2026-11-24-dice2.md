## 1. What was built

### 1.1 The referee's keyed dice (games/dq0/dice.inc, the skeleton's dice_key)

- **A request's dice are a function of its key alone.** Before each request is answered, the skeleton (rf/rf.c) calls
  `dice_key (slot)`, which hashes the key into dice.h's generator state (xs_seed); the request's dice are the
  generator's from there, as before. The key:
  - the game's seed and its dice offset (dq0 -R, the hub's dice);
  - the asker's slot (the world's, the UI's, the denizens');
  - the request's kind, actor, other, a (a test's purpose, a blow's aim, a harm's cause) and b (a test's how);
  - its moment: the hub's clock; a blow's own moment (RS_ATTACK's when, a denizen's intent's); a creature's sheet
    (RS_NEW_ACTOR) its record's number in place of a moment and of its place in the hub's table, so the same creature
    has the same sheet whenever it's met;
  - n: how many requests at that moment shared the key's tag (3 bits of the hash) before it. Two requests are never one
    roll unless they are the same act at the same moment.
- **The hash:** four Pearson lanes (a fixed shuffle of 0-255; each byte goes through the four lanes in turn, each lane
  T[itself ^ the byte as the lane before left it], the first lane after the last), then two bytes more. Every bit of
  the key reaches every bit of the state, and not linearly: xorshift's own step is linear, so keys a fixed mask apart
  would roll dice a fixed mask apart. The lanes as the seed and offset leave them are kept once (the hub's dkey.base).
  A JS model (N:/.claude/.../dice2/tools/hashq2.js) checked 200,000 keys 100 tenths apart: d100 chi-square 111 (99
  degrees of freedom), lag-1 correlation -0.002, the same roll as the last 1.00% of the time (1% fair); 8 actors at
  one moment agree 1.02% (1%); 100,000 keys of 2,000 moments x 50 counts: no state repeated; each state bit 49.7-50.3%
  ones. The game's own hashing was checked against the model in the emulator (tools/dkcheck.js: the keys of a game's
  start and first requests, in all three slots, a creature's sheet among them: the referee's xs_seed after each equal
  to the model's).
- **Where it lives.** In assembly; dq0harn's overlay 9 (its hash table and code), its stub (6 bytes: ovcall's) and its
  reader and writer of the hub (dk_rd, dk_wr: the overlay can't map the hub) in main RAM; dq0null all in main RAM. What
  it keeps is the hub's (hub.h's struct dkey, the referee's: the counts' moment, the 8 counts, the base, own). The old
  per-asker streams (dice_to, its 13 bytes of state) are gone.
- **A test's own stream.** RS_DICE's seed (t_rules, dq0rules) sets dkey.own: no keys for the rest of the referee's life,
  so the unit tests' seeded cases roll as they did. dq0rules passes unchanged.
- **Left as they were:** the bearings' own dice (bdice: now the bearings' count of tests, begun from a key), the
  venoms' and healing's own (vseed), an appraisal's dice by the thing (JUDGE_IN), moving quietly's mix (QUIET_IN: with
  every request keyed it no longer matters but to a test's own stream). The appraisal's dice in the UI (way.c) were
  already keyed by the thing.

### 1.2 G14, the quiet dice: ended

A denizen's RS_TEST without RS_ASKER mixed its dice from the game's stream as it stood, so while nothing else rolled
the same roll came again (dq0mine's dwarf: 35 critical failures). Now every request's dice are its key's: a dig spell
each 10 minutes is a test at its own moment; two tests at one moment (a search's every hidden actor, every looker at
one hidden) differ by their count. dq0mine's miner now rolls, over 12 spells, CF, MS, CF, MS x6, CF, MS, MS (denB's
sample; at -R 1: MF, MF, CS, MF, MF, CS, CS, MS, CF, MF, MS). OPEN.md's G14 is taken out.

### 1.3 The minds' and the world's keyed dice

- **The minds** (games/dq0d/mkey.s, called from dq0d.c): each decision's dice keyed by the game's seed and offset, the
  denizen's number (the hub's actor's did), the moment it decides at (its ready, not when the minds come to it) and a
  repeat count; the minds' own work at a moment (what they hear, the fights, the character's ways with one) by the
  moment. A denizen's choices no longer move with another's. The populations and lairs' placing keep their own
  stratum-seeded dice (the dungeon's).
- **The world** (games/dq0w/wdice.c, part wdice): where a thing let fall lies (the character's, a denizen's weapon), a
  missile's stray, its flight past its mark and whether it's lost, each keyed by the seed, the offset, the moment and
  what (WD_FALL, WD_DROP, WD_SHOT and the thing or the squares). Its rolls go through wroll (main RAM), which a test's
  rig can set.
- Both mix with multiplies or sums whose carries make them not linear; checked with JS models the same way (chi-square
  96-117, the same as the last 0.98-1.02%, actors agreeing 0.95-1.02%, no repeats).

### 1.4 A rigged-dice switch for tests (hub.h's struct rig; dq0harn)

- A test's scene sets a table of up to 8 entries in the hub (the referee's region: the scene builder's SCN_HUB).
  Each entry: a request kind, its a (purpose, aim, cause) or any, its actor or any, its uses or every time, and up to
  4 faces. The first entry that matches a request, with uses left, has that request's first dice show its faces (the
  referee's RS_DICE hook, fixed); the rest fall by the key. An entry with no faces keeps a request from the entries
  after it.
- HarnMaster's d100 test shows a critical success at 5, a marginal one at 1, a marginal failure at 99 and a critical
  one at 100, whatever the mastery; tests/lib/dq0.js's `grade: 'well' | 'pass' | 'fail' | 'badly'` says so.
- Kind WORLD: the world's own rolls (a its WD_* purpose), the faces the rolls' values plus 1, every time.
- In tests: `sc.rig([...])` in a scene's call, or a dq0Scene spec's `rig: [...]` (tests/lib/dq0.js's sceneRig).
- Test-only: no game sets it, no menu shows it; with an empty table the referee's code does what it did (one byte of
  the table read a request). The real referees' rules are untouched; dq0null hasn't the switch.

## 5. Assumptions

- **The dice are the engineer's** (WORLD-MODEL.md section 8: "how they are drawn, provided every test is fair and
  independent"). The key's parts, the hash and where its state lives are mine; the checks above are the fairness.
- **A creature's sheet by its record, not by when it's met** (P1 realism: a creature is who it is; P2: the same for
  every actor). Its sheet was made from the denizens' stream at its first meeting; now from its record's number.
- **The same act at the same moment is one roll, any other two are two** (G14's own words; section 8's "independent").
- **The dungeon's dice untouched** (section 9: every seed's dungeon is the user's).
