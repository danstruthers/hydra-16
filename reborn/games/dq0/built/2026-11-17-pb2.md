## What was built

All in dq0c and its tables; nothing the player sees is different.

- **The walk's witnesses** (commandcompile.js's roleWitness; the host's walk prunes the same): for each rule with a role
  in it, words one of which any span it gives a role in holds (a role's name: its first words, if they're all words;
  else the nearest item of its sequence that can't be empty).  The walk goes into such a rule only if its span holds
  one ("the whetstone" holds none of `<object>`'s and, my, of, your: not walked into).  The index has the words (59),
  the sets and each rule's set, and flags the words in its dictionary (LX.witness), so the walk looks up those tokens'
  numbers alone.
- **A sequence of one item is in the grammar table as that item**: `[ <manner> ]` an option of the name, `"a" | "b"`
  an alternation of words.  869 of the 1,205 sequences; the rules 2.6K smaller, each saving an e_seq.
- **child's quick ways**: a rule goes straight to e_nt's cache (not through ends, then leaf again); an option gives its
  position and its child's ends (a leaf's or a rule's) without e_opt.
- **The Bloom filter**: two bits a word (w & 63, and (w >> 3) + 2 (w >> 6), mod 64), so the corpus's words are let into
  a quarter as many rules as with one ("from" no longer into `<lies>`); its rows moved from dq0c's RAM to after the
  rules in their bank (2,040 bytes given back), and the rules' cache made 256 entries with some of them (the walk's
  rules no longer lost to a collision: "put" re-entered `<extinguish>` for 4,300 cycles).
- **The walk spared**: an option with tokens is walked into straight (its child can match them, as it can); an
  alternation, sequence or repeat of words alone isn't walked (no name in it, no role); a sequence item's start, when
  one position alone can be it, is taken without a reach.
- **The meaning's lookups by halves**: the verbs of several words grouped by their first word (each group's verbs in
  their order, so the first that fits is the same), the line's first word's group found by a binary search; a verb's
  preposition or turn found by a binary search of the pairs, in verb order.

Tables: the grammar version 10 (the Bloom rows after the rules), the index version 4 (the witnesses; the Bloom rows
gone; LX.witness), the words' table version 5 (the groups).
