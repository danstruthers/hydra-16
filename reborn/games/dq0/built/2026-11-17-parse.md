# The parser's quick fixes, and the play test's parser bugs (2026-11-17; as built, for review)

GAME-CRITIQUE.md's section 10, phase A: the quick parser fixes (its 2.2 and 2.6) and the play test's parser bugs (its
section 1).  Branch a-parse, from a46c5c38.  Kept small, and in the direction of 2.8: phase B's grammar that records
roles (the user's choice, DECISIONS.md's "The critique's questions: the user's answers") replaces the scan these
fixes patch.

## What was built, and why

- **The other thing's phrase keeps its head** (2.2).  The meaning's scan (dq0c's cmdmean.s, and its host twin,
  sim/tools/commandwords.js) kept the second noun phrase's *first* word; it keeps its last now, as the first phrase
  does ("with the iron key": the key, not "iron").  Two rules go with it, so the head is the phrase's own:
  - *its phrase ends at a word of how* after its word ("attack the rat with the dagger in my left hand": the dagger,
    not "hand"; "take the torch from the sack in my left hand": the sack);
  - *a part of the place after the first thing and its preposition is the other's kind*, not the first's ("put the
    torch on the door": the torch, the door the other; "tie the rope to the pillar"), but not in a request ("ask the
    dwarf to open the door": the door the errand's, errand.c reads `obj.kind`) nor for the way out (the puzzle's, the
    "way out" pair's: "use the seal on the exit").  A part's own word goes with its kind ("on the bars").
  - The host twin and the assembly agree on the corpus and the meaning lines (1,024, the job's lines added to
    cmdmatch/lines.txt).  The `--mean` check had stopped running (its sim65 program 27 bytes too big for the sim's
    64K): it runs its lines in parts now, as the matcher's check runs the corpus.
- **The other thing's kind, in the resolver and the table.**  resolve.c's side-pocket rule reads each thing's own
  kind (`side_pocket`: "take the chisel from the side pocket" has the pocket as the other's now).  actions.c's table
  has a new takes flag, `TK_ONTO` (put, hang, tie): a part of the place as where a thing's put or tied is refused
  plainly, "You can't put the torch on the door." (2.8 step 7), but a hole's with "in" (a shaft's, a pit's: a thing
  let fall in it, as before).  Drop isn't refused ("drop the dagger on the stairs" drops it, as before).
- **"... what?" to a part of the place** (2.6).  refuse.c's `o_unknown`: `U_WHAT` with a kind named and words after
  the verb is `U_CANT` ("You can't push the wall.", not "Push the wall what?"): push, pull, taste, use, drop.  And
  "name" alone asks of its own line (names.c set `cl`: it said the last command's line, "Look what?").
- **"open" with a word that names nothing** (2.6).  hthings.c's `H_SHUT`: no thing of that name that closes says
  "You see no drawer here." (the door ahead worked before); "lock" alone ("unlock the lock", "force the lock") is the
  door's still.
- **"drop all"** (2.6).  parts.c (overlay 6), as the command's parsed: the line made "drop" and the letters of what's
  in its hands, back in the queue (letters.c's a line each, each put down as one named).  *The choice*: what's
  carried loose (in no hand, worn by none, in nothing) goes too, as "each thing the verb could apply to" (2.8 step 7);
  nothing worn, nothing in another (a pack, a sack, hung from a belt).  The world has no loose things now (each is
  held, worn or in something), so it's what's in the hands.  None: "Your hands are empty."
- **"take all from the case"** (the play test's "#17").  The resolver's "take all" (what lies about, each by a
  handle's id: the "#17" the world was asked to find in the case) isn't for a take from a thing; the world's own count
  is (its name none, its count all): the chooser (things.c's `pick`, part 4) takes "" in a thing as any in it, not in
  a hidden compartment (found or not); the count's words, `many` (part 105): "You take everything from the case."
  (one thing: "You take a chisel from the case."; some left, the hands and pack full: "You take what you can from the
  case."); none: "You find nothing in the case."
- **"on the shelves"** (the play test).  things.c's `in_other`: not found on a thing that holds things on it (a
  surface, not a container; or a compartment named whose prep is on) is `SA_NOT_ON`, "You find no dagger on the
  table." (items.h's enum's end: past 127, so the world's `say` writes `SAY_AT2` for it now).  `SA.not_in` and
  `SA.not_on` say "You find nothing in/on the case." with no name.
- **"Which one?" letters** (the play test's three adders all (G)).  which.c gave each choice the capital of the last
  thing it found *on its square*: several on one square all had the last one's.  The resolver gives each choice its
  place in the sight record (resolve.c's `ci`, kept through the sort; which_w's byte 21), and which.c takes its
  capital from that.  Two on one square no longer say ", on the left/right" (both said "on the right").
- **"combine the tablet and the seal"** (the play test).  parts.c split it at "and" into two commands, each "Combine
  what with what?"; a line begun with combine or join is one line now, the parser's the one with the other.  The
  host's reference (commandcompile.js's `phraseSem`) reads it so too.
- **"pick the lock"** (the play test: the help offers it).  The grammar's `<unlock>`: "pick the lock", "pick lock"
  (no "of the chest") the one in front, as "unlock" names it; the world's rule chooses (a key that fits first, then
  the lockpicks: wdoor.c's).
- **`say hello`** (the user's answer, DECISIONS.md's "The command grammar's answers": refused with the form shown).
  The grammar's no is said by refuse.c now (`A_SENSE`, said.txt's NO row: "I don't understand that.", the same
  words); a say with no quotes (a word after it, no quote typed) shows its form: 'Put the words in quotes: say
  "hello".' ('whisper "go away"' for "whisper go away").
- **"eat" alone** (the play test; "drink" alone works).  The grammar's `<eat>` takes no thing: a meal from what it
  carries, as feed.c's eat with none named ("You're not hungry.", "You eat a meal.").
