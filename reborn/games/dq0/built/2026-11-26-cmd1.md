# cmd1: the fixed cost of a command (2026-11-26; as built, for review)

From 3249c653 (even2's merge, its images). The brief: the work every command pays from its Enter to the world taking
its intent, profiled by part, and cut at its causes for every command at once; then every measure past 90% brought
under it in all three layouts; OPEN.md brought up to date.

Commits: 4abfb789 (the gate), 8ecc9050 (hub_mem), 7d48da7a (I_RISE), 465e00d7 (the foreground's calls), 24f62aea
(hub_map), 17ee0fee (dq0pcfelled's re-aim), and this report.

## 1. The fixed cost, profiled

The tool: scratch cmd1/lph.js, on even5's lp3.js (lineprof with --span: the window from the Enter to the test's own first
letter, the test's words, not lineprof's '*'). Each instruction in the window is charged to a task and to a phase, the
phases cut by marks in the run itself:

| phase | from | what's in it |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| wake | the Enter | the UI asleep on its nap; the others' work meanwhile |
| echo | the UI's t_getkey | the line into the story: said_again, parts_in, alias_said, the echo's say, its write, the console |
| take | the UI's line_free | ui_cmd, fore_sync (the foreground's kernel calls), letters_in, cq_post |
| events | the parser's request posted (cq 0 to 1) | events, heard, the status line |
| parse | the UI's t_line | names_in, alias, pacing; the parser's work (dq0c); cq_wait |
| command | the parser's reply taken (cq 2 to 0) | resolve and the command's own handling, to the intent sent (iq_head) or the UI's own answer |
| gate | the intent sent | to the world taking it (iq_tail) |
| answer | the intent taken | to the first letter |

world's) at each pace, dq0letwhich's "F" (turns, menacing, played) and dq0pcfelled's "d" (turns, menacing, normal),

What it shows:
- **Enter to the parse taken is 150,000-200,000 cycles** (21-28% of D17) for every command; even4's 300,000-400,000
  was to the intent sent, the command's own handling with it.
- The single largest pieces of it: **the echo** (50K: the console's VT and serial work 23K, the UI's 18K), **the
  parse** (43K: dq0c's own 19K for "sit" and "inventory", up to 143K for "walk to the door ahead to my left" and 150K
  for "put the whetstone in the sack", lpall.js's B0-all-turns.txt), **the take** (40K: fore_sync's five TASKFORE
  calls and their GETPID, the world's pass the foreground gave it).
- The windows are CPU-bound: the kernel's idle is near nothing, so work moved earlier or later inside a window wins
  nothing; only work removed, or a wait removed, does.
- **One wait**: the gate. At the real paces the world takes an intent only once the UI has seen to the news (main's
  take loop: ui_first's hold, ui_eq and ui_dq). The UI wrote those at its pass's end, after its command, its part
  copies back and the prompt's line; nothing rang the world then, and a world that had looked as the intent was sent
  slept its 8 ticks (143,000 cycles). dq0answer's "played sit": its intent sent at 230,710, taken at 487,197.
- **The part copies**: in the UI, a handler in another part than commands (sit's s_limbs_cmd in part news, look's
  room in part where, the doors' reach_go in part missiles, look ahead's look_way) copies part commands back (47,582
  cycles) for its return alone: 9 of dq0answer's 28 commands at the turns pace (B0-all-turns.txt); at the turns pace
  that copy holds ui_giving and the world with it. In the world, part intent_more for I_RISE: copied in, then part
  legs, then intent_more back (63,666).
- The call overheads: hub_mem (the hub from an overlay's code: 28 calls in an inventory's answer, 16,146 cycles with
  its bytes), hub_map (the world's 280 calls in a take's answer, 12,577).

## 2. What was cut

1. **The gate** (4abfb789). At the real paces (tpr) the UI writes ui_eq and ui_dq as events() and heard() return (the
   only places they change in a pass), and rings the world if an intent waits in the ring. The turns pace as it was
   (the lockstep reads them as what the UI has done about the news). "played sit" 716,593 -> 528,144 by itself.
2. **hub_mem in assembly** (8ecc9050): to and from left on the C stack where memcpy takes them, the window kept on the
   6502's stack: some 170 cycles a call less, 9 bytes of the UI's main RAM back.
3. **I_RISE straight to part legs** (7d48da7a): intent() (main RAM) calls legs for a position, the intent kept in
   lw_m as intent_more keeps it; hiding oneself goes on to intent_more. Every "sit", "stand", "kneel": 45,000 cycles
   of copies the less.
4. **The foreground's kernel calls** (465e00d7): o_fore_set tells the kernel only of the tasks whose place changed
   (fored kept by it as it's called; the map's own foreground keeps what it puts back). Two calls at an Enter where
   there were five and a GETPID.
5. **hub_map in assembly** (24f62aea) for the UI, the world and the minds (hub.c's HUB_ASM_MAP): some 25 cycles a call
   where its C took some 65.

Tried and not built:
- **A command's last call made after its part has returned** (act's go_after, generalized: s_self_do's H_LIMBS and
  H_LOOK, s_places_do's look_way): 47,582 cycles off sit, stand, look, look ahead at every pace. It needs some 30
  bytes of the UI's main RAM, which has 78 over its floor and no 64 to spare (the headroom rule); part commands has 76
  free. The lead, with the room for it found first (section 7).
- The parser (dq0c's matcher, 19-150K a command), the console's echo (23K), the background's turn (the kernel's
  FORE_RUN: every 8 ticks the background's, 3-60K a window): each a cost of every command, each its own work (section 7).
