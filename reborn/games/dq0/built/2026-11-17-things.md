### Coins to the purse

The purse is the first pouch hanging from the character's belt that the coins fit (`s_purse`, part 10: a container
that hangs, of no family, open, with room).  Coins (a lot of small things counted: pennies, farthings, crowns) go there
when they're taken up or taken from another thing (`s_carry`: before the pack and before a hand), stowed with no place
named (`s_stow_where`: unasked), and when the hands' rule frees the hand that holds them (`home_of`); put there by the
hands' rule they join the coins there (part 35's `join`, now `s_join`, through a stub).  No pouch: as before.

### A light left burning taken up again as a walk starts

The light the hands' rule set down burning (laid at the character's feet, or set in a sconce: `left_lit`) is taken up
into a free hand as a walk begins (a walk, a step, "go to", a take's walk over, a journey: dq0w.c's `intent` asks part
90's `s_lit_back`), a second's work, told before the walk: "You take up the torch. You set off south."  No hand free
and the walk begun: "You set off south. You leave the torch behind." (a step's words of their own, `SV_LEAVE`: hub.h,
said.txt's `@steps`, task.c's `doing[]`).  With a hand free it's taken up as the walk is asked, whether the walk is
then refused or not; with none, a walk refused ("You can't go that way.") leaves it remembered.  It's forgotten once
taken up or left behind, or if it's been moved, put out or taken in hand meanwhile.

### Nothing battered with a light

A blow with nothing named at a door, a window or a portcullis counts a light in hand (a torch, a lantern, a candle; lit
or not) as an empty hand: the world's IO_HANDS answer says which hands hold lights (its `ireq.mat`), and the UI's
`s_wield` (choose.c, part 9) leaves them out of the referee's RS_WIELD.  The game's existing rules then choose, as for
an empty hand:
- the other hand's thing, if there is one (the referee's choice, as ever): "You set to battering the door with the
  bar.";
- else a better tool carried: the question as ever, "You can try battering it with your hands, but would you rather use
  your pick?";
- else, or "no": the bare hands, the free fist, `SK_PUNCH`: "You set to punching the door."

**The choice:** the fist.  The game's rule for a blow with nothing at hand is the bare hands, its fists; the shoulder
isn't one of the referee's blows (a punch, a kick, a head butt), and asking is already the rule when a better tool is
carried.  The fist is named (`SK_PUNCH`), not `SK_BEST` as bare hands were: with a torch held the referee's best would
take the torch as its weapon.  A light named ("break the door with the lantern") is still used (open question 2).  At a
denizen a torch fights as ever.

## Open questions

4. A climb (stairs, a ladder, a rope) isn't a walk for the light's taking up.
5. A small thing stowed with only the pack asks between the pack and its side pocket (the user's rule: more than one
   place, asked).  Should the pocket be offered only beside other places?
6. In a fight the home-first rule puts the weapon away before the light (the sword stowed for the rations, the torch
   kept).  Should a weapon in a fight be kept in hand before the light?
7. A "stop" typed while a fight has taken over a reach's step stops the fight, not the reach: the reach goes on after
   it (dq0reachflee's scene showed it once the hands' order changed its steps; the test now has the dagger sheathed
   first, as the old order's torch was laid down first, and its stop lands on the pack's coming off as before).  Seen,
   not changed.

   (dq0busyloop: the unlit lantern stowed for a second torch, the lit one kept).  Should an unlit light keep its hand
   too?
9. The thing a tool is for is kept from the rule only when its request names it (`handled`): the torch lit, the blade
   sharpened, the light filled or dipped, the rope tied, the grapple.  Three things for two hands (a rope tied to a
