The user (2026-11-17, DECISIONS.md's "The one-path plan and phase A's questions"): "Rethink the D17 speed targets.
Please investigate the assumptions there and reset those that do not make much sense."  Branch d17, from bc75b074,

## What was built, and why

### The investigation

**The clock (3.58 MHz?)** The board's CPU clock is J7's 3,579,545 Hz (docs/02-hardware/architecture.md's Clocks;
base/include/hw.inc's `CLK_CPS`).  J8's 7.16 MHz fails the RAM tests with the stated parts (quirks.md: "3.58 MHz is
the fastest valid speed").  The emulator runs the same clock (base/sim/run.js's `CLOCK`, 3.579545).  So 720,000
cycles are 0.201 s and 1,790,000 are 0.500 s.  The assumption holds.

**The line (115,200 baud?)** It's 111,861 baud: the ACIA's clock is 1.790 MHz, not the 1.8432 MHz its rates are made
for, so every rate is 2.9% slow (quirks.md).  The serial driver sends each character from VIA timer 2, with two idle
bits after it at 115,200 (base/lib/serial.inc's `RATE 7`): 12 bits of 32 cycles, **384 cycles a character, 9,322 a
second**.  What that costs the player:
- *before a first letter*: the line typed drawn into the story and the cursor's moves, 25-66 bytes (dq0answer's look,
  inventory, look ahead, turn left): 10,000-25,000 cycles, 1-4% of the target, and mostly sent while the parser works;
- *a look about*: 383 bytes from its first letter to the prompt (371 letters), 147,000 cycles on the line, a fifth
  of its 700,000 after its first letter; the rest is the UI wording it (said.c), 80,000-150,000 cycles a sentence;
- *a full screen*: 1,760 characters, 0.19 s.

At 115,200 the text is never held up by the line.  At 9,600 (11 bits, 4,224 cycles a character) the same look's 383
bytes take 1.6M on the line alone: a look in half a second can't be had at 9,600, as D17 said.

**What the player waits on** (each answer's pauses, with every task's cycles in them: a scratch profile of
dq0answer and dq0lookfar by task, 50,000-cycle buckets):

| Wait | Where the cycles go | Size |
|---|---|---|
| The first letter | the UI's parse and resolve (dq0c), its first words (said.c); for a step, the world getting it under way; for a count's drop, the world's split; for an attack, the fight | 180,000-660,000; drop 1g 2.75M; attack 2.2M-3.7M |
| A look one way's words, after "You peer ahead." | the senses' look (59% of the pause at the turns pace, 44% at the normal), the UI, the background | 3.2M (turns), 4.8M-5.0M (normal) |
| The look across | the maker's chunk in the foreground (31%), the denizens and the world | 14M-21M |
| The map's rows, after its first words | the maker drawing the whole map first (69%) | 5.0M-5.3M |
| What's carried, after "You carry" | the world's list (78%) | 860,000-930,000 |
| A turn's or a step's new view | the senses (40-56%) | 1.2M-3.7M |
| A reach's steps, without the tests' -J (the game as played) | the world (60-87% of each pause) | 2M-11M a step |
| A dig with a knife, at the turns pace | the storage task, the card (59%) | 9.7M |

So after its first words an answer may wait on the senses, the maker, the world or the card, as well as on the game's
own time (a walk, a step, a listen's three seconds).

**First letter against whole.** The first letter is the acknowledgement: the player sees the command was heard.
The whole (to the last letter before the prompt) is not a measure of speed:
- it grows with the words (a look about's 370 letters; a cavern's would be several times that) and with the ground
  seen (the look across makes a chunk);
- at the real paces it takes in the game's own time (a walk to the door 15.6M, a step's 3.3M) and news told before
  the prompt (examine the torch's whole was 6.1M: "Something stirs." five million cycles after its answer);
- at the turns pace it takes in the slice's turn for the denizens (a turn's whole 6.6M-7.6M).

What the player feels after the first letter is whether the words keep coming.  A look that flows for a second is
read as quickly as it comes; one that stops for a second after its first words is slow.  So the measure is the
longest pause between two letters of the answer's own words.

**The turns pace against the normal pace.**  At the normal pace the background (the denizens' minds, the senses, the
world's own work) shares the CPU, and a first letter swings 10-25% with where an Enter falls against it (turn left
448,000-544,000 over eight phases).  The turns pace is quieter but not still (sit 577,000-731,000).  Both are held:
the normal pace is the game as played, and the turns pace isolates the command's own path.  The relaxed and menacing
paces aren't measured (open question 7).

**How each measure was taken, and whether it was reliable.**
- *The waits dropped the test switches.*  DQ0ANS_WAIT 1 and 2, and parse2's jitter, put the waits before the dq0
  line too, and the runner adds its switches (-a, soft questions answered yes; -J, the reach in one lump; -I, -N, -F
  ...) only to a dq0 line typed straight after its prompt wait (sim/test.js's asksOf and the rest).  Those phases
  played another game: soft questions asked ("Keep walking?") and answered by the next line ("You stop walking."), the
  reach's steps with a line typed while they went on ("put the whetstone in the sack" 787,000), the turns pace's "take
  the sack from the backpack" 32.9M where wait 0's was 2.8M.  That was parse2's open question 8 (and its eight-phase
  table's waits 1 and 2 and jitters were partly that other game).  The want lists had grown "You stop" to let those
  lines through.
- *One typing is a sample.*  The answer as typically had is the median of a few typings at different moments; one
  typing can sit 10-25% from it.

### The new targets

In tests/lib/trends.js's `D17`, at 115,200 baud and 3.58 MHz:

1. **A command's first letter within 720,000 cycles** (a fifth of a second), for every command, looks and the map
   included.  Held in dq0answer (28 commands, both paces), dq0lookfar (5), dq0jobs (32), dq0hold (1), and the
   designators' tests and their kin (dq0torchwords, dq0letters, dq0letcmd, dq0letsight, dq0letwhich, dq0letseveral,
   dq0letstow, dq0doff, dq0letdens): every line they time.
2. **A reading's longest pause within 1,790,000 cycles** (half a second: the user's "a look in half"): from its first
   letter to its last, no two letters further apart.  A reading is an answer whose words are its content, with no game
   time to wait for: a look about, a look one way or across, the map, what's carried, a thing examined or assessed.
   The pause ends at the prompt or at the world's news told after the answer began (tests/lib/dq0.js's `NEWS`:
   "Something stirs", "Nothing stirs in sight", a "You hear" not "You hear nothing"), whose wait is the game's time.
   Held in dq0answer (look about twice, look ahead, inventory, examine the torch, assess the sack, both paces) and
   dq0lookfar (the three looks ahead, the map).
3. **None let off.**  trends.js's `target` fails a measure over its target, whatever its trend says; the line lists
   are gone; `sim/tools/trends.js --update`'s `--accept-over` is gone with them.
4. **Measured so:**
   - dq0answer types each pace's game three times, dq0lookfar its game three times, each line after 0, 1 or 2 waits of
     2M cycles by its typing's own pattern (dq0.js's `dq0Waits`, DQ0ANS_WAIT turning the patterns on for the lead's
     tier); the median of the three is held and is the trend, each typing's value is in the notes, and the worst is
     said when it's over and the median isn't;
   - no wait goes before a dq0 line, so the switches stay on in every typing;
   - dq0Timer reads the UI's `busy` as each CR comes, and dq0answer and dq0lookfar fail on a timed line typed while
     the character's busy (held till it's free, or a soft question's answer), since its first letter would be the
     game's time.  Shown on three runs with the old waits put back (scratch jitter seeds 4, 6, 7): seed 4's "drop the
     sack" came at "Keep putting on the backpack?" ("You stop putting on the backpack.") and failed so;
   - the nine 9,600-baud tests type `echo b115200 >/dev/serctl` first.
5. **The whole** of each look and of the map is kept as a trend (tests/trends.json), its rise said, never failed.

## Open questions

1. **An attack's first words.**  An attack says nothing until the blow lands (2.2M-3.7M), and its status line, "Fighting
   the giant rat", comes 1.5M after Enter.  Should it say its first words as it begins, as a walk or a reach does ("You
   close with the giant rat.")?  The words are the user's to choose.
2. **A count's first words.**  "drop 1g" ("You put down a penny.") waits on the world's split of the stack: first
   words at once, as a plain drop's?
3. **A look one way's words.**  The senses' look takes about 2M cycles of its own before any of it is said.  Should it
   tell the near part first and go on, or be made quicker?
4. **The look across.**  A chunk made in the foreground is 14M-21M: should the maker make the chunks ahead of the eyes
   in the background, or should a look into ground not yet made say what it can first?
5. **The map's rows.**  The maker draws the whole map (3.7M of the maker's own) before its first row: row by row as it's
   drawn?
6. **The status line as an acknowledgement.**  The measure is the story's first letter (HOUSE.md's "it says a word as it
   begins").  Should a status line that changes at once count?
7. **The other paces.**  Only the turns and normal paces are timed; the menacing pace's background is the busiest.
   Should one of dq0answer's typings be at the menacing pace?
8. **The game as played.**  The tests' switches (-J, the reach in one lump; -a, soft questions answered yes; -I, -N,
   -F ...) make the timed game not quite the player's.  Without -J, a reach's steps each cost the world 2M-11M cycles
   ("take the sack from the backpack" 33M at the turns pace, 40M at the normal): no D17 target sees it, since a reach's
   steps are the game's time as well.  Should the actions' outcomes have a target (at the turns pace, where every pause
   is the CPU's), or should dq0answer time a typing without -J?
