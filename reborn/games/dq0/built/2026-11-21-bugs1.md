## 1. A flight typed between two engaged steps of a reach (reach1's open question 2)

**The fault, found:** a "flee" typed between two engaged steps was held, with an I_STOP sent though there was nothing
to stop: the step under way was a round's action, with no long task. The world then sent two E_READYs, the round's
and the stop's. The UI took the flight at the first one (I_DISENGAGE, busy). The second came after it and made the
character free again, so a prompt showed and the next line typed ("inventory") was answered at once, before the
flight's words. Seen by recording the events and intents by cycle: I_STOP 387.77M, E_READY 389.82M, E_READY 389.99M,
I_DISENGAGE 390.02M, a prompt at 390.38M.

**The fix (main.c):** a line that breaks off between two steps, with no long task and no fight, sends no I_STOP. The
round's own E_READY is the line's. A fight taken over, or a long task, is stopped as before.

**Tests:**
- dq0reachflee2 (new): "flee" typed 1M and 2M cycles after the torch's step. It checks that the reach stops with no
  further step, the pack is still worn, and the flight comes before the next line's answer. It failed in both games
  before the fix and passes now.
- dq0stopsweep's scene was re-set. Its stop is no longer let in between the keys of a line being typed. In this tree
  the reach ended before the 22.5M try, and "inventstop" was typed. After that change, as before, a later line typed
  first gets no stop. The truth checks are unchanged.

## 3. A kill to a task inside a kernel call (sched1)

K_SCHED_NOTED (sched.s) now looks at a frame in page 0's kernel code that has a note pending. The look runs with IRQs
on; IRQs are off only for a moment while the kernel task's zero page is read. If the frame is at a system call's last
instruction back to the program (an RTS, or a CLC or SEC before one), with the program's return address above the
frame (below $DF00), the frame's PC is set to K_NOTE_RETURN with the frame's carry. The call then ends there through
the notes, as K_NOTE_CHECK ends one. TICKS's tick, taken just after its IRQs-off time, lands exactly there.

**Test:** t_note has 8 children spinning on TICKS alone (t_child's new "S"), each killed a tick later than the last.
Each must be killed (137) within a tick of the one spinning in its own code. On the old kernel it fails (a kill
waited longer); on this one it passes.

**Other checks:**
- schedcheck on note: 0 model mismatches, 0 invariant breaks (the pick is unchanged).
- The base's 17 tests pass.
- IRQs off at most 192 in the suite.

## 6. HyBASIC's / that leaves a fraction (bmath1)

tests/speed/small.bas has two new stretches: X = I / H (H = 1000, even) and X = I / K (K = 1001, odd).

The pretest tried: a remainder is sure when a bit of the dividend's low byte is set below the divisor's lowest set
bit. Then, with no FN next, the library is called at once. Cycles a pass (sim/speed.js), before -> after (FN looked
at first):

| Stretch | Before | After |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| I / H (1000) | 10,874 | 10,062 (1.08x) |
| I / K (1001) | 15,131 | 15,163 |
| I / G (3) | 5,400 | 5,443 |
| FIX(I / G) | 1,537 | 1,550 |
| INT(-I / G) | 1,890 | 1,903 |
| I * G / G (whole) | 2,064 | 2,103 |

It wins only for even divisors. An odd 16-bit divisor, the usual case, gains nothing. Every other integer / pays 13-40
cycles, and FIX(a / b), mandel-ex's whole work, pays 0.8%. **Not kept:** run.inc is as it was. sim/bench.js and the
Mandelbrots weren't re-run, since nothing changed in BASIC.
