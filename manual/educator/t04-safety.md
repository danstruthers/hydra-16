# Topic 4. Safety

The keyboard lessons need no more care than any computer room. This topic is about the bench: hot irons, molten
metal, flux fumes, sharp offcuts, and mains-powered supplies. None of it is exotic, and all of it is managed in
school workshops every day. But it has to be managed on purpose.

> **NOTE:** This topic is a starting point for your own risk assessment. It isn't one. Your school, district or
> institution has rules for hot work, for chemicals and for electrical equipment, and they come first. Take this
> list to whoever is responsible for workshop safety where you are, before you order anything.

## The hazards, and what to do about each

| Hazard | How it hurts | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **The iron's tip**, 350 °C with leaded solder and 370 °C with lead-free | Burns, in an instant | A stand at every station, used every time. Irons carried nowhere: they stay at the bench. Never passed hand to hand. Cables routed away from elbows. Off, and five minutes to cool, before anyone packs up |
| **Molten solder** | Flicks and splashes: burns, and the eyes | Safety glasses on everyone at a bench, and on anyone watching. Never shake or flick an iron. Wet sponges kept damp, not dripping: water on a hot tip spits |
| **Clipped leads** | They fly | Glasses again. Hold the lead while cutting it, or cut into a cupped hand or a box |
| **Flux fumes** | Irritate eyes and lungs; can sensitise, and are a known trigger for asthma | Extraction at each bench, or strong cross-ventilation. Heads to the side of the work, not over it. Ask about asthma before the first session |
| **Lead**, in tin-lead solder | A poison if swallowed: by way of fingers and food | **Use lead-free solder.** With any solder: no food or drink at the bench, and hands washed before leaving the room |
| **Isopropyl alcohol** | Flammable; irritates | You hold the bottle. Small amounts, on a brush, with every iron off and cool. Never near a hot tip |
| **The power supply** | Mains voltage inside its case | Cases never opened. Supplies inspected before use: no cracked cases, no damaged leads. The supply unplugged, not just switched off, whenever hands are on the board |
| **A capacitor fitted backwards** | It can burst when the power goes on | The Companion's Unit 2 has every one double-checked. Glasses on for every first power-up, and faces back |
| **Sharp ends** under the board | Scratches | Leads clipped flush. Boards carried by their edges |
| **Static**, to the chips rather than the people | Dead chips, found a week later | A wrist strap for whoever fits chips. Chips stay in their foam until Unit 3 |

## The rules, for the wall

The Companion's Unit 1 has the students tick and sign these. Put them on the wall as well.

1. Glasses on before the iron is on.
2. The iron lives in its stand.
3. If it falls, let it fall.
4. Nothing to eat or drink at the bench.
5. Hair back, sleeves up.
6. Power unplugged before hands touch the board.
7. Wash your hands before you leave.
8. A burn goes under cold running water for ten minutes, and you tell me.

| ☐ | Check |

| ☐ | A written risk assessment exists, and whoever must sign it has |
| ☐ | Parents or guardians have been told, where that's required, and any asthma or relevant condition is known to you |
| ☐ | You have soldered a board yourself, recently, with the same irons and the same solder |
| ☐ | Every iron has a stand, and every stand is stable |
| ☐ | The extraction works, and is pointed the right way |
| ☐ | There are glasses for everyone, and for a visitor |
| ☐ | You know where the cold tap, the first-aid kit, the fire extinguisher and the isolating switch are |
| ☐ | You know how many students you can supervise at once (below) |

## How many at once

Soldering needs closer supervision than a keyboard lesson. A workable rule: **one adult who can solder for every
eight students at benches**, and no student at a bench out of an adult's sight. With a full class and one teacher,
that means a rotation: eight at the benches, the rest at keyboards doing a lab, changing over at half time. The
year plan in Topic 2 (Shape D) is built on that rotation, and Topic 6 has the details.

Younger students (12 and 13) can solder well, and do. Give them the through-hole resistors, capacitors and
sockets; an adult does the surface-mount RAM chips, the first power test, and anything involving the alcohol.

## When something happens

| What | Do |
|---|---|
| A burn | Cool running water, at once, for at least ten minutes. No ice, no creams. Then your institution's first-aid procedure. Anything bigger than a coin, or blistered, is seen by a first-aider or a nurse |
| Solder or a clipping in an eye | Don't rub. Your institution's eye-injury procedure, at once |
| A student feels unwell in the fumes | Out of the room, into fresh air. Stop the session and improve the ventilation before anyone goes on |
| A component smokes or bursts at power-on | Power off at the wall. Everyone back. Let it cool. Then it's a fault-finding lesson: see what it was, and why |
| The smell of burning, and you can't see where | Power off at the wall, and find it before anything goes on again |

Record every incident, however small, the way your institution asks. And talk about it with the class afterwards:
a near miss that's been discussed is worth ten rules on the wall.

## The quieter risks

* **Hurry.** Almost every burn, bridge and backwards capacitor happens in the last ten minutes of a session. Stop
  soldering fifteen minutes before the end, every time, and use the time for inspection and the build log.
* **Pride.** A student who has made a mistake will sometimes hide it. Make finding faults the celebrated thing:
  the Companion's inspection pages ask "what did the inspection find? (Everybody finds something.)" Mean it.
* **One expert.** In every group, one student soon does all the soldering. Rotate the iron on a timer.
