# About This Guide

This guide is for whoever is leading other people through the Hydra-16: a teacher with a class, a professor with a
lab section, a club leader, or a parent with one determined child. It doesn't teach the Hydra. The *User's and
Builder's Manual* does that. This guide is about teaching *with* it: what to cover, in what order, how long it
takes, what goes wrong, how to keep a room safe, and how to tell whether anyone learned anything.

## The three books

| Book | Who holds it | What it's for |
|---|---|---|
| **The User's and Builder's Manual** | Everyone | The tutorial and the reference. Every fact is in here, and only here. |
| **The Student Companion** | Each student | A workbook: the build log, 26 lab sheets, 8 projects, reference cards, a logbook. It has questions, and no answers. |
| **The Educator's Course Guide** | You | Course plans, notes on each lesson, safety, assessment, and the answers. |

Wherever this guide says **Chapter 6** or **section 6.4**, it means the manual's. **Lab 3**, **Project 2**, **Unit 4**
and **Card 5** are the Companion's. This guide's own chapters are called **Topics**.

## What you need to know first

Less than you might fear. The manual was written so that a twelve-year-old can follow it alone, and you can keep
a chapter ahead of a class. But do these three things before the first lesson:

1. **Do Labs 1 to 3 yourself**, on the same computers the students will use. An hour and a half, and you'll have
   met every snag they will.
2. **Read the manual's Chapter 6** properly. It's the one chapter everything else stands on.
3. **Decide how the Hydra will be there** in the room: emulated, built already, or built by the class. Topic 3
   compares them. It's the decision that shapes everything else.

If you're going to build, also solder something yourself, and read Topic 4 and Topic 6 before you order anything.

## What this guide won't tell you

**What a kit costs, and where to get one.** That changes, and it isn't in the Hydra's repository. Topic 3 lists
what a kit is made of, so that you can price it; the manual's Appendix I is the full parts list.

**Which standards your course meets.** Those are yours to map. Topic 1 lists what the Hydra teaches, grouped the
way most computing curricula group things, to make the mapping quick.

## How the answers were made

Every transcript in this guide's answers was typed into a Hydra, the same one the manual used (three RAM modules,
the sample SD card, no Vera X, no clock chip), and what's printed is what it said. When your students' Hydras
differ from that one, their answers will differ in the same small ways that the manual's section "This book's
Hydra" lists. A different answer isn't always a wrong one: check the *reasoning*.

> **NOTE:** Keep this guide out of the students' hands, and not only because of the answers. Half of what a lab
> teaches is the frustration that comes just before the answer.
