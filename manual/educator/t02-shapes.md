# Topic 2. Course Shapes

Nobody teaches all of it. The manual is a year's reading, and the Companion's labs and projects are about sixty
hours at a keyboard, before any soldering. This topic gives you the pieces and their sizes, and then six ways of
putting them together, from a single week to a full year.

## The pieces

**The build** (the Companion's Part One) is 15 to 20 hours of bench time for one board, however many hands share
it, plus the memory card. Topic 6 says how to share it.

## Shape A: a week (emulator only)

A holiday camp, or an activity week: five days, four 75-minute sessions a day. No soldering, no hardware. Ages 12
and up.

| Day | Morning | Afternoon |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Meet it | Lab 1; Lab 2 | Lab 3 (both sessions) |
| 2. Command it | Lab 5; Lab 9 | Lab 6; Project 4 begun |
| 3. Program it | Lab 20; Lab 21 | Lab 21; Project 1 begun |
| 4. Make it sing and draw | Lab 12; Project 3 | Lab 13; free choice of project |
| 5. Finish | Projects | Lab 4, as a whole-group demonstration; a showcase for visitors |

The week has one language (HyBASIC), three projects to choose from, and something to show at the end of it. Lab 4
comes last on purpose: after a week of *using* tasks and files, the explanation of how it all starts lands on
prepared ground.

Let students choose their main language in week 11, after a taster lesson of each (the manual's section 6.9, and
Chapter 25, are made for this). A class with two or three languages in it makes Lab 23 come alive.

## Shape D: a year, with a build

The full course: a Hydra built by the class, and then used for everything. Three terms. Ages 14 to 18, in groups
of two or three to a board.

| Term | Bench (one long session a week) | Keyboard (one or two lessons a week) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The build: the Companion's Units 1 to 5. Ten to twelve weeks at 90 minutes | On the emulator: Labs 1 to 9, so that students can *use* a Hydra by the day theirs first starts |
| 2 | Hardware projects: Lab 14 Part A; Project 6; an SD card reader for each board | Devices and numbers: Labs 10 to 15; then a language: two of Labs 16 to 21 |
| 3 | Free: a second memory card, a case, a front panel | Labs 22 to 26; a major project of each student's choice; the showcase |

The emulator in term 1 is not a stopgap. It means that the first evening a group's board prints `POST ok`, they
already know what to type at it.

## Shape E: a club

One 90-minute meeting a week, with no end date and members of mixed age who come and go. Don't run a course: run
a workshop.

* **One build at a time**, on the big table, shared by whoever is there: Topic 6.
* **The lab sheets as badges.** Each member works through them at their own speed, and gets the Companion's Skills
  Passport signed as they go. Older members sign for younger ones.
* **A project wall.** Everyone always has a project on the go, and shows it when it's done.
* **A demonstration to open each meeting**, five minutes, by a member: one thing I found out.

## Shape F: a university laboratory

Six three-hour laboratory sessions beside a second-year course in computer organisation or operating systems.
The students can program already. The Hydra's value to them is that the *whole* system is small enough to read:
after the labs, the kernel's source is the further reading.

| Session | Theme | Read | Do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A small machine, whole | Ch 1, 7, 26 | Labs 4 and 24; trace the reset sequence in section 7.4 against the boot log |
| 2 | Banked memory and protection | Ch 7; Appendix F | Map a task's address space by experiment: `/proc`'s `mem` and `regs` (section 17.8); explain what stops one task reaching another's RAM |
| 3 | Tasks, scheduling and shared state | Ch 7, 17; Appendix K | The samples `race`, `chorus`, `philo` and `prodcons`; measure the scheduler's time slice; write the race, and its fix, in assembly |
| 4 | Names: everything is a file | Ch 9, 12, 17 | Labs 7, 10 and 14 Part B; build a private namespace; compare with Unix mount tables and with Plan 9 |
| 5 | The machine interface | Ch 27; Appendix K | Lab 25; a filter (like the sample `upper`) written against the system calls directly |
| 6 | Seeing inside | Ch 28, 29 | Lab 26; the bug hunt of section 29.8; then read how `db` is itself only a user of `/proc` (29.11) |

A good assessed exercise to end on: *choose one design decision in HydraOS, find it in the source, and argue for
or against it.* Sixteen fixed tasks, bank registers on the zero page, text control files, per-task namespaces:
each is a decision with a cost, and the source is short enough to find them in.

## Choosing

| If you have | Take |
|---|---|
| A week, and no hardware | Shape A |
| A half-term, and a syllabus to cover | Shape B |
| A semester, and freedom | Shape C |
| A year, a workshop, and a budget | Shape D |
| No timetable at all | Shape E |
| Students who can already program | Shape F, or Shape C at double speed |

Whatever the shape, keep three things: **Lab 3 early** (nothing works without it), **a project for every student**
(it's what they'll remember), and **a showcase at the end** (it's why they'll finish).
