## The safety rules

Read section 3.1. Then tick each rule, to say that you understand it and will keep it.

| ☐ | The rule |
|---|---|
| ☐ | I wear safety glasses whenever I solder or clip leads. |
| ☐ | The iron is in its stand whenever it isn't in my hand. I never leave it switched on when I walk away. |
| ☐ | I work where the air moves, and I keep my face out of the smoke. |
| ☐ | I don't eat or drink at the bench, and I wash my hands when I finish. |
| ☐ | Long hair is tied back, and my sleeves are out of the way. |
| ☐ | If the iron falls, I let it fall. |
| ☐ | The power supply is unplugged whenever I solder, fit a chip or move a jumper. |
| ☐ | If I burn myself: cool running water for ten minutes, and I tell an adult. |

## Practice first

Don't make your first joint on the Hydra. On a scrap board or a practice kit, make joints until they look like the
good one in the manual's Figure 3-3, and then make a mistake on purpose and repair it.

| ☐ | Practice | How many | Checked by |
|---|---|---|---|
| ☐ | Resistors soldered and their leads clipped | 10 | |
| ☐ | A chip socket soldered: one corner first, checked, then the rest | 1 | |
| ☐ | A solder bridge made on purpose, and cleared with braid | 1 | |
| ☐ | A part taken out again without lifting a pad | 1 | |
