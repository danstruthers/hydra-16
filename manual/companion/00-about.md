# How to Use This Companion

This is a book to write in. It goes with the *Hydra-16 User's and Builder's Manual*: the manual explains, and this
companion is where you do things and keep a record of what you did. Keep the two side by side.

Wherever this companion says **Chapter 6** or **section 6.4**, it means that chapter or section of the manual. Read
it first. The companion doesn't repeat what the manual says.

## What's in it

**Part One, the Build Log**, is for builders. It has a box to tick for every group of parts in the manual's
Chapters 3 to 5, places to write down what you measured, and a chart for finding faults. If you're using the
emulator and not building a Hydra, skip it.

**Part Two, the Lab Sheets**, is for everyone. Each lab goes with a chapter of the manual, takes one sitting, and
ends with a few questions. They work on a real Hydra and on the emulator, except where a lab says otherwise.

**Part Three, the Projects**, are bigger pieces of work: a program to type in, get going, and then make your own.

**Part Four, the Reference Cards**, are single sheets to keep open beside the keyboard: the keys, the commands, each
language, and the processor's instructions.

**Part Five, Your Record**, is a logbook, and a list of the skills you've gained, to tick off as you go.

## The signs

| Sign | Means |
|---|---|
| ☐ | A box to tick when you've done something, or checked it |
| ✎ | Write your answer on the lines |
| ★ | A challenge: harder, and optional |
| ⚠ | Take care: read the manual's caution before you go on |

## Good habits

* **Write what you see, not what you expected.** If the Hydra says something different from the manual, write that
  down. It's the most interesting thing that can happen in a lab. The manual's section "This book's Hydra" lists the
  differences that are normal.
* **Date every entry.** A build takes weeks, and you will forget which evening you fitted the capacitors.
* **Save your work where it's kept** (section 6.7). At the end of every lab: is it on the SD card?
* **When you're stuck, write the question down** before you ask for help. Half the time, writing it answers it.

> **NOTE:** There are no answers in this book. Your teacher has them, in the *Educator's Course Guide*. If you're
> working on your own, the Hydra has them: nearly every question here can be settled by trying it.
